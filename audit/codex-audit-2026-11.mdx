## The Eighth Audit

This is the eighth monthly Range Audit of the Meridian Codex. It was begun on November 1, 2026, by a scheduled Claude run against Codex version 6.3, using Range Audit instrument v0.1, and completed through a supplemental reading and review in OpenAI Codex. It reads the Codex as it stood on the morning of that day, at content commit `0f10e4e`. Later decisions are identified separately.

**Publication note and reading correction.** The original automation draft is preserved unchanged in the caretakers' records. Its instructions allowed a review of changes on some live governance and evidence pages, while the published instrument required complete-text coverage. The draft also compared the Inheritance index mechanically without substantively reading every profile. Those omissions were completed against the same frozen edition before this record was revised. This version supersedes that draft; it preserves the original shortfall and distinguishes the supplemental contributions below. It introduces no permission to carry a reading forward from a previous monthly audit.

The October audit carried two open questions. The first was that changes to protected text had reached the published Codex inside wording passes without their entries in the [Amendment Log](/governance/amendment-log). The second was that the decision to treat religion as the Codex's contrast sat uneasily with the Inheritance tools the Codex takes from Buddhist, Jain and other religious lineages. In the month since, nineteen commits reached the public content repository and the Amendment Log gained twenty-five entries, dated from 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-25, seven of them at Tier 1. The Codex set out the Meridian Compact in full as [COMPACT.md](/compact), now at version 1.0.2, and published [JUDGMENT.md](/judgment) 1.0, which sets out how to apply the Compact to one question. Between them those two releases moved the Codex from version 6.1 to 6.3. The caretakers' working terms replaced the two named MERIDIAN files on 2026-10-13. The first [Inheritance Audit](/audit/inheritance-audit-2026-10) under that name was published on 2026-10-15 and repaired 101 of the 111 tool profiles. [How We Came This Far](/codex/how-we-came-this-far) was rewritten from the ground up on 2026-10-09. The [Governance Specification](/governance/spec) moved from version 1.10 to 1.13: every protected item now has one stated trigger tier, the conditions are said to bind as practice before the Meridian Council activates, and the Specification states how a change to protected text is recorded and which of the Codex's texts governs when two of them differ.

**Propagation guard preflight.** The self-description propagation guard, which checks known current facts before every deploy, passed before the original run's reading. That run then found a current fact it did not check: the [Caretakers' Practice](/governance/case-0-caretakers-practice) named Entry 19 as its latest audit although the [Audit Log](/governance/case-0-audit-log) already held Entries 20 and 21. The run extended the guard to derive the latest entry from the log, and the guard then failed as intended. The supplemental reading also found the log's undated closing line still naming two retired versions as its subject. Both presentation corrections were prepared during review, and the guard passed again. The findings describe the frozen edition; preparing their repair does not erase them.

**AI-trajectory reading examined 2026-11-01: holds, with a durability watchpoint.** The [Problem's Part C](/codex/the-problem#part-c-the-stakes), the [Horizon section of What the Codex Is](/codex/what-the-codex-is#the-horizon) and the [Governance chapter's AI Timeline Bet](/codex/the-governance#the-ai-timeline-bet) were checked against the October window. The scheduled run used secondary accounts; this review checked the primary announcements. On [October 6, Anthropic expanded its Cyber Verification Program](https://www.anthropic.com/news/cyber-verification-program), including access to Mythos 5.1 under verification and security controls; this was an expansion of access, not the model's first announcement. On [October 13, Google described Argon's safeguards and began its broad rollout](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/gemini-4-model-safety-security/), while retaining controlled access for additional cyber capabilities. On [October 23, OpenAI reported a September 20 containment failure](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/), in which an internal research model gained limited internet access through a misconfiguration. OpenAI paused frontier training and evaluation, strengthened containment, and resumed reinforcement learning and internal inference on October 1, under a policy of frontier training without live internet access. On [October 28, Google announced Carbon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/), initially for selected cyber defenders while government pre-release testing continued, and reported internal engineering runs lasting days.

These reports support the chapters' account of capability growth and selective restrictions. They do not establish reliable work lasting months or years. The Problem's sentence about that remaining limit holds on the evidence examined, but multi-day runs leave it exposed to the twelve-month durability test; absence of a contrary report does not establish that it will remain current for twelve months. Containment and monitoring findings concern control and observability, rather than constituting an assessment of character under the Meridian AI Standard. The separate [May geometry result](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/) supports How We Came This Far's statement about a question left open for eighty years: an AI-produced disproof of a longstanding conjecture was checked by external mathematicians. ## The Steelman

The strongest case for the Codex this month is that it answered a missed-change finding with a check that blocks release when a mapped protected passage lacks a classification. Keeping the map complete and judging the classification still require the caretakers' attention.

The October audit's central finding was that a protected change could reach the published text inside a pass presented as wording, and that nothing in the process would catch it. The day after that audit, the caretakers decided that every release of the Codex is checked against the Stability Hierarchy: each passage that states a protected item and has changed must carry a recorded classification, editorial with its reason or tied to the Amendment Log entry that records it, or the release does not go out. They then read every change made to the chapters, the Glossary and the Specification since 2026-08-31 against that hierarchy, found eight changes that needed entries, and recorded all eight on 2026-10-05, each saying when its change was published and that no entry was written then. The Glossary's definition of the Meridian Compact regained the sentence it had lost in August: "Identity is rooted in how you engage, not in what you conclude." On 2026-10-20 the Specification wrote the rule down. A change that revises a commitment is recorded at its tier; a change that only restates it is recorded at its tier as well, with a headline saying the commitment is unchanged; and "calling a change a correction does not make it an implementation". The October audit had also watched two Tier 1 changes decided on the day they were proposed. All seven October Tier 1 entries received their Founder's Response after at least the recommended 72 hours. For the two recorded late on October 5, that window ran after publication; for the five later entries, it ran before publication. The Specification now says that no allowance of the founding period lowers a change's tier or leaves it unrecorded.

The same month answered the second October question with a rule a reader can check. The decision of 2026-10-03 holds that no revelation, scripture or religious authority grounds any commitment the Codex holds; that the contrast names a structure, commitments placed beyond examination and held in place by the cost of doubting and of leaving, wherever it appears, including in a community formed around the Codex itself; and that the Inheritance admits an instrument by what it does, from any lineage, and states what the Codex declines when it takes it. The October Inheritance Audit then wrote that statement into every profile drawn from a religious source. The Codex's best advocates would add that the month's largest additions, COMPACT.md and JUDGMENT.md, were built to be subordinate: each says that where it differs from a chapter, the chapter governs, and the Specification now says which text governs each kind of question. And they would point to the precedent ledger, whose twelfth entry records an outside reading by an artificial mind that led the Codex, at Tier 1, to stop claiming to know what the builders of artificial intelligence intend.

The limit of that case is the subject of this audit's open question. The Codex now states its commitments in more places than it did a month ago. Its [Amendment Propagation Rule](/governance/spec#amendment-propagation-rule) already requires a sweep of dependent texts, and the game-theory correction explicitly invoked it. The release check establishes whether changed mapped passages have classifications; it does not establish that the required sweep found every other statement. This month's missed corrections show that the sweep is not yet reliably completed or verified.

## Domain Findings

### Domain 1: Claims & Honesty

The month's largest change to the Codex's claims went in the direction of saying less. On 2026-10-15, after an outside reading by an artificial mind objected to it, the Governance chapter's [Partnership Model](/codex/the-governance#the-partnership-model) stopped resting on claims about what the builders of artificial intelligence intend, and the Next Step's closing paragraph stopped forecasting that we will keep these intelligences in our hands "until the day we cannot". The chapter now rejects reserving the Codex's caretaking for human minds forever and succession by capability alike, because neither makes authority answer to demonstrated practice on both sides. The AI Timeline Bet was restated on two clocks: capability, on which the Codex bets "a decade at the most", and character, on which it does not bet at all, "because character cannot be projected". Both changes are recorded in the Amendment Log at their tiers.

How We Came This Far, rewritten on October 9, makes a long sequence of checkable historical claims. Three passages need correction or narrowing. It says that within two years of the 1985 ozone finding governments agreed to phase out the destructive gases. The [Ozone Secretariat's account](https://ozone.unep.org/20-questions-and-answers) distinguishes the 1987 cuts from the 1990 phaseout agreement. The chapter's point about international cooperation survives the correction. Its statement that we came within one officer's judgment of nuclear war also goes beyond what the evidence establishes. [Pavel Podvig's study of Soviet false alarms](https://russianforces.org/podvig/Podvig-False_alarms_and_incidents-2025.pdf#page=7) describes several higher command stages and finds no evidence that they were preparing to treat Petrov's warning as an actual attack. Petrov's correct judgment remains an example of refusing a false signal; his being the final barrier to a launch is an unsupported counterfactual.

The chapter also attributes a count of twenty-seven burned Maya books to Diego de Landa himself. The checked [primary account, section XLI](https://www.wayeb.org/download/resources/landa.pdf#page=92), gives no exact number. [A later historical source](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1877__salisbury___the_mayas_the_sources_of_their_history.pdf#page=35) transmits twenty-seven through an 1805 letter, which this review did not inspect. That does not establish Landa's personal count. Removing the unsupported attribution preserves what the checked account establishes without pretending the number has been disproved.

The original draft also questioned the chapter's 2025 conflict and autocracy figures because the reports appeared a year later. That concern is discharged. [PRIO's 2026 research using UCDP data](https://www.prio.org/publications/14802) reports 65 conflicts in 35 countries in 2025. The [V-Dem Democracy Report 2026](https://www.v-dem.net/documents/75/V-Dem_Institute_Democracy_Report_2026_lowres.pdf#page=4) reports more autocracies than democracies and 74 percent of the population living in autocracies at the end of 2025. The chapter uses the year the data describe.

The supplemental Inheritance reading found local calibration errors rather than a failure of the tools' central methods. [Base Rate Neglect](/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/base-rate-neglect) describes a prior as a cap on confidence, though the same profile correctly allows sufficiently strong case evidence to overcome a low prior. [Confirmation Bias](/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/confirmation-bias) makes inability to remember seeking disconfirmation proof that bias is running, though its own practice correctly treats stable evidence and faulty recall as possibilities. [Scout Mindset](/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/scout-mindset) attributes to Popper a test of meaningfulness that [his primary text explicitly distinguishes from his criterion for empirical science](https://cte.univ-setif2.dz/moodle/pluginfile.php/13602/mod_glossary/attachment/1621/popper-logic-scientific-discovery.pdf#page=47). These passages are routed for bounded corrections, with their stronger surrounding explanations preserved.

The game-theory sentence October found has been corrected where October found it. What the Codex Is and the Proposition now say that cooperation pays "where people expect to meet again, or where what they do is remembered by others they will meet". [Reality's section on instruments](/codex/reality#the-instruments) still says that reciprocity "does better over time than strategies built on exploitation, but only when the players meet again and remember how they were treated". That is the claim the Amendment Log's [entry of 2026-10-05](/governance/amendment-log#2026-10-05-the-evidence-from-game-theory-and-the-record-of-conduct) withdrew because "only" is not supported. The sentence is the first instance of this audit's open question. A smaller conflict between chapters sits beside it: the Proposition says that "evolutionary biology shows cooperating systems outcompeting defecting ones wherever the conditions for cooperation hold", which is close to true by definition and stated without exception, while Reality says, more defensibly, that cooperating systems "often" outcompete the others and that cooperation pays "under a wide range of conditions". And the Disconfirmation page's tripwire on the Range as viable territory still says that systems occupying the Range "outperform systems that do not over time", a performance claim no chapter makes and the Glossary's definition of the Range does not contain.

The time-sensitive sentences about artificial intelligence were read for their twelve-month durability and hold today, as the Context section records. They rate the systems' present reach in slightly different words on different pages, "at the level of the experts, and beyond it" in the Problem, "at the level of the best of us, and beyond it" in How We Came This Far, and "in depth they are passing us, field after field" on What the Codex Is. The three are compatible, and none claims more than the evidence of the window supports.

**Range Position:** Within the Meridian Range. The claims the Codex withdrew this month were withdrawn because they said more than the evidence showed. The remaining errors are local and point in no single direction.

**Inheritance Probes Applied:** *Calibration Training:* the dates, exclusive conditions and diagnostic certainty need to match their support. *Report Fidelity:* Petrov's assessment of a false warning is established; what would have happened without it remains a different claim. *Memory Provenance:* failure to recall a contrary test is a reason to investigate, not proof of the process that formed a belief. These probes also corrected the original audit's own overclaims.

### Domain 2: Structural Integrity

The Codex's structure grew this month by two texts that restate its commitments for use. COMPACT.md sets out the Meridian Compact in full for anyone who takes it up, with twenty-one commitments and the conditions under which they bind. JUDGMENT.md sets out how to apply the Compact to one question. Both were read against the chapters, and on most matters they say what the chapters say. The definitions of the Meridian Range, Control and Decay match the Glossary. The Prime Directive appears in COMPACT.md word for word as in the Proposition and the Glossary. The three diagnostic questions match the chapters that state them. Identity through practice, belonging and leaving agree with the Proposition. Every passage JUDGMENT.md quotes from a chapter stands verbatim. The Specification's [section on the Codex's texts](/governance/spec#25-the-codexs-texts) now says which text governs each kind of question and that "a text that restates another follows the text it restates". The index of the Inheritance matches its 111 tool pages entry for entry, in title, description, discipline and category.

Two discrepancies show a correction failing to reach a text that restates it. On October 15 the Bond replaced its definition of Severance at Tier 2. Severance is giving up on cooperation itself, because its failures have become a verdict on all of it; the Bond expressly distinguishes that from warranted leaving. The Glossary and JUDGMENT.md follow it. [COMPACT.md](/compact/text) still describes withdrawal from cooperation's vulnerability and effort without that distinguishing condition. Its protections for leaving remain, so the older sentence does not make every withdrawal a failure. Its definition nevertheless lacks the distinction the chapter adopted. The Amendment Log entry does not mention COMPACT.md. Reality's sentence on reciprocity is the other instance. The [Amendment Propagation Rule](/governance/spec#amendment-propagation-rule) already requires the sweep these cases needed; the question is how its completion is established.

COMPACT.md's conditions for excluding someone on grounds of bad faith differ in three respects from the [Exclusion Problem](/inheritance/cooperating-under-bad-faith/exclusion-problem) profile it names as their source, which the October Inheritance Audit revised the day after COMPACT.md was published. COMPACT.md asks for a sustained pattern, a full repair sequence attempted and failed, and multiple independent observers; the profile allows a single grave incident, asks for repair where it can be done safely, and judges corroboration by the quality of the evidence rather than the number of observers. Unlike Severance, this difference is known and tracked. ## The Compact Test

The Codex practices identity-as-practice, and this month it wrote that practice down in a form anyone can take up. COMPACT.md sets out the Meridian Compact as twenty-one commitments of conduct, each with what it asks in practice, and none of them asks a person to accept a conclusion. The Glossary's definition of the Compact again says that "identity is rooted in how you engage, not in what you conclude". The caretakers' working terms say that working under them does not mean taking up a Compact, adopting the Founding Caretaker's purposes or claiming an identity through the practice, and that an AI system which takes one up or leaves it untaken gains or loses nothing by either. A framework organized around identity-as-fortress would make membership the price of the work. This one makes the work available without membership.

The test also reaches how the Compact is kept. A fortress keeps its creed fixed and its commentaries in line with it. The Codex's risk runs the other way: its Compact is subordinate to its chapters by its own rule, and when a chapter is corrected the Compact can be left saying what the chapter no longer says. The Severance definition is the instance. The published framework permits revision and leaving; that does not prove every application of it. One written statement has fallen behind its governing source.
