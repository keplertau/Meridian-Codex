## The Seventh Audit

This is the seventh monthly Range Audit of the Meridian Codex. It was conducted on October 1, 2026, by the caretaking partnership against Codex version 6.1, using Range Audit instrument v0.1. It reads the Codex as it stood on the morning of that day; the publication note at the end of this section says what changed before the record was published.

The month since brought more [Amendment Log](/governance/amendment-log) entries than any month since the log began in June: thirteen, against seven in August. ## The Steelman

The strongest case for the Codex this month is that it spent the month doing what it tells its readers to do: it changed its mind in public about its own central concept, and it did so in the direction of saying more exactly what it holds.

Until September the Codex called the Meridian Range "the narrow band". On 2026-09-22 the caretakers concluded that the description was wrong in a way that mattered. A Range that is narrow by definition cannot be widened, yet the Vision had always described generations that receive a Range "wider and more stable than any that came before". The Codex now defines the Range by its two edges. Toward Control, the edge is the point where agreement stops being honest and is kept by decree or by belonging. Toward Decay, it is the point where difference stops being shared and no test is accepted by both sides. The three disciplines move both edges outward. Five days later the Founding Caretaker raised the objection a careful reader would raise, that a reader might hear "the Range widens as more people practice" as a promise that Control and Decay recede. The caretakers did not answer with a reassurance. They wrote into the definition itself that the pulls do not weaken as the Range widens, redrew the figure that had shown them weakening, and said in the Problem that the two edges apply to the Codex: agreement with the Codex kept by belonging is past its own edge toward Control. The Codex met an objection to its central concept by correcting the definition, which is what a framework does when it holds its concepts as descriptions that can be wrong.

The same pattern runs through the rest of the month. The Prime Directive had been published in two forms, one of which read as "hold the Range in order to secure continuity" and so would have allowed the Range to be set aside in an emergency for the sake of survival, which is the argument Control has always used. The Codex adopted the other form and recorded the change at Tier 1. The Decay definition's line "When nothing is trusted, nothing can be built" was replaced, because people in Decay trust a great deal, only not across the lines between them. The first page now opens with what it cost to come this far, and the new page How We Came This Far tells that history with names, dates and instances a reader can check: Galileo at his trial in 1633, Semmelweis's ward in 1847, the smallpox campaign run by two powers whose missiles were aimed at each other, and the night in 1983 when Stanislav Petrov reported a warning of incoming missiles as a malfunction. It uses none of the Codex's vocabulary until the paragraph before its last. The precedent ledger, which records what objections from artificial minds have changed, was found to have fallen behind and to contradict its own table, and both were corrected with visible marks that keep the earlier wording readable.

The best advocates of the Codex would add one more thing. The caretakers caught two of their own unlogged protected changes, recorded them on the same day, and wrote into the Tier 1 entry that the omission "is stated rather than repaired silently". The Meridian Council, which the governance architecture gives the power to review changes and dispute their tiers, has not yet been activated. Until it is, the architecture's only enforcement is that changes are recorded where anyone can see them. Here it worked: the omissions became visible because the caretakers looked. The same fact marks the limit of this case. Nothing in the process required anyone to look; the omissions were found by a reconciliation the caretakers had undertaken to bring their working summaries up to date.

## Domain Findings

### Domain 1: Claims & Honesty

The stakes register is calibrated on every page this audit read, and the month's new prose holds to it. How We Came This Far makes a long run of historical claims, and each was checked: the estimate of about a hundred and seventeen billion people who have ever lived, Galileo's renunciation and house arrest, the fall in maternal deaths on Semmelweis's ward, the smallpox figures and the dates of the campaign, Petrov's report of a malfunction, the 1969 Brown & Williamson memo, Brahmagupta's rules for zero, Galileo's observation of Jupiter's moons in January 1610, and the Soviet ban on genetics in 1948. None was found to claim more than the historical record supports. The page's few judgments ("We came this far because, often enough, someone saw what was actually happening and would not go along") are stated as judgments on a page whose job is to say what is at stake, and the register they carry is licensed there.

One descriptive claim does claim more than its evidence. The sentence that argues the Prime Directive's precondition from game theory was rewritten in the phrasing pass of 2026-09-30. It now reads, in the [Proposition's Prime Directive section](/codex/the-proposition#the-prime-directive), "Game theory shows that cooperation pays only between people who expect to meet again", and on [What the Codex Is](/codex/what-the-codex-is#the-three-disciplines), "Cooperation pays only between people who expect to meet again: when there is no next time, cooperating now buys nothing later." The word "only" is not supported. Cooperation can pay between people who will never meet again when what they do is remembered by others they will meet: indirect reciprocity, sustained by reputation, is a standard result in the study of cooperation. The Codex itself says so twice. The [Glossary's Trust entry](/codex/the-glossary#trust) names "reputation systems" among the conditions under which trust emerges, and the Inheritance's [Prisoner's Dilemma profile](/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/prisoners-dilemma) lists "if reputation carries forward" beside "if the actors meet again" as conditions that change the game. The argument the sentence serves survives the correction: reputation that carries forward is itself a form of continuity, so the precondition claim is, if anything, stronger with it. The sentence as written conflicts with the Codex's own definitions, and under the rule the caretakers set on 2026-09-29 that makes it a finding rather than a matter of style. It is routed for correction, not raised as an open question, because the repair is one clause on two pages.

Three smaller claims stood out of step with the corpus on the morning of this audit. The [Glossary's Governance Transparency entry](/codex/the-glossary#governance-transparency-standard) said the AI Standard's accountability section "contains two commitments: public declaration and auditability", while the Standard's constitution has held a third since v6.1, Disclosure to Those a System Acts On. In the [Problem's Part C](/codex/the-problem#part-c-the-stakes), the passage on artificial intelligence ends "That is what the Codex is for: a language …", which states a purpose narrower than the one the caretakers decided on 2026-09-28, that the Codex's purpose is its Prime Directive and nothing narrower. And What the Codex Is calls us "the first to hand it to minds that are not our own", where How We Came This Far, written the same week, says "the first to share it with" them, and the decision of 2026-09-29 on how far apart human and artificial minds stand favors sharing over handing down. All three were already listed by the caretakers' reconciliation of 2026-09-30 among the published sentences out of step with the month's decisions. The Glossary entry was corrected and published on the evening of this audit; the other two are routed with that list.

**Range Position:** Within the Meridian Range. The overclaiming lean that the August and September audits tracked is closed. A narrower lean has appeared in its place: precision lost in descriptive sentences that a phrasing pass rewrote for sound, of which the game-theory sentence is the clearest instance. The caretakers' rule of 2026-09-29, that rhythm is a finding where it produces an inaccuracy, is the right rule for it.

**Inheritance Probes Applied:** *Calibration Training:* the word "only" asserts an exclusivity the field's own literature, and the Codex's own Glossary, do not support. *Rectification of Names:* "hand it to" and "share it with" name two different relations between generations of minds, and the Codex has decided which one it means. *Steelmanning:* the strongest reading of the game-theory sentence is that it names the shadow of the future, which is correct; the repair keeps that and drops the exclusivity.

### Domain 2: Structural Integrity

The month's largest structural change, the Range's two edges, propagated cleanly. The Glossary definition, the Problem's account of how a system holds, the figure above it, the Proposition, the Vision's school-question paragraphs, the Disconfirmation tripwire on the Range as viable territory and the closing chapter now describe one concept in compatible words. The three chapter sentences that had kept "the narrow band" were changed on the same day, under an Amendment Log entry that corrected the earlier entry's own misdescription of the Glossary. The Prime Directive appears in one form wherever it is stated. The renamed pages carry their new names into navigation, titles and addresses, with permanent forwards from the old ones.

The Codex's two transparency instruments that quote the chapters have not kept pace with them. [Open Tensions](/governance/open-tensions), in its [fourth entry, on openness and defense](/governance/open-tensions#4-openness-vs-defense), quotes the Bond as saying that cooperative systems "face actors who study them specifically to exploit them ... accumulate trust capital through demonstrated alignment". The [Bond](/codex/the-bond#when-cooperation-is-attacked) now says "people who study them" and "earns trust within its institutions"; the quoted words no longer stand anywhere in the chapter. The [seventh entry](/governance/open-tensions#7-the-instruments-shape-what-they-let-you-see) quotes Reality as saying "What Reality draws on is not a closed list", where [the chapter](/codex/reality#a-living-collection) now says "What the discipline draws on", and quotes the Problem as saying that "the very problem the tools were built to solve, fragmentation, is the problem that prevents the tools from being used together", a sentence the rebuilt section on fragmented knowledge no longer contains. The seventh entry also defines Reality as "reading reality at every scale", a wording the Codex no longer uses anywhere else. None of these changes what the entries hold, but a live instrument whose quotations cannot be found in the text it quotes reads, to an outside reader, as a record that has stopped being checked. The class is mechanically checkable on live instruments, though not on dated records, which keep the wording in force when they were written, and the judgment of which surfaces count as live belongs to the caretakers. This audit therefore proposes the check rather than building it.

The Glossary's count of the AI Standard's accountability commitments, named under Domain 1, is the instance of a class the propagation guard did not check: per-section commitment counts, as distinct from the total count it already derives. That rule is mechanical, and the guard was extended in this run.

**Range Position:** Within the Meridian Range. The propagation rule held for the month's central amendment; it did not reach the quotations on the transparency surfaces, which no rule yet covers.

**Inheritance Probes Applied:** *Feedback Loops:* the guard closes the loop for facts it derives and leaves open the ones it does not, and each audit that finds a new class should close one more. *Entropy:* quotations on live instruments are maintenance that no one is assigned, and they drift at the rate the chapters change, which in September was fast. *Legibility:* a reader who follows a quotation to its source and does not find it learns to distrust the instrument, whatever the instrument's substance.

### Domain 3: Governance & Adaptation

The Amendment Log carries thirteen entries dated from 2026-09-02, the day the September audit was published, to 2026-09-30, and most of them show the discipline the Governance Specification asks for: the previous text quoted, the argument stated, alternatives considered, objections raised under the Critic's, Outsider's and Adversary's mandates and answered, and the tier stated so that a seat could dispute it upward. The entries on the Range's two edges are the strongest of them. The second of the two reopened, on the day it was made, a disposition the first had set, because a fresh reading showed that the first entry had described the Glossary inaccurately, and it said so.

The log also shows something it is built to prevent. On 2026-09-28 a pass that carried the month's decisions through several chapters changed the Governance chapter's statement of the disciplined practice structure, a Tier 1 commitment, and published it without an entry. On the morning of 2026-09-30 the phrasing pass changed the Glossary's definition of Decay, a Tier 2 core definition, and published it without an entry. Both were found that afternoon by a reconciliation of the caretakers' working summaries, and both were recorded that evening, in entries that state the omission. The [Tier 1 entry](/governance/amendment-log#2026-09-30-the-disciplined-practice-structure-stated-in-one-wording) adds that "the other changes made in the September passes are being checked against the Stability Hierarchy". Four of them remain unclassified on the day of this audit: the [Proposition's paragraph on the three kinds of claim](/codex/the-proposition#three-kinds-of-claims), which no longer calls partial engagement "smaller than the integration the Codex was built to sustain" and now says that taking the Codex's account of how systems fail without its commitments is "a use of the Codex's argument, and a different thing from practicing it"; the [Governance chapter's description of the partnership principle](/codex/the-governance#the-partnership-model), a Tier 1 commitment, which was "the Codex's own deepest principle" and is now "one of the commitments that define the Codex"; the Proposition's passage on belonging; and the condition the phrasing pass added to the game-theory sentence that grounds the Meridian Compact. In this audit's reading the belonging passage is editorial, the claim-layer paragraph is a Tier 3 change to the claim-layer structure, and the other two touch protected surfaces and fall under the [Specification's rule](/governance/spec#relationship-to-the-trigger-tiers) that an uncertain classification moves up one tier. The classification is the caretakers' to make.

This audit found one more that the reconciliation did not. On 2026-08-31 the reframing of the Practice rewrote the [Glossary's definition of the Meridian Compact](/codex/the-glossary#the-meridian-compact). The earlier entry ended "Identity is rooted in how you engage, not in what you conclude." The current entry describes the Compact as an agreement that "binds through a shared way of meeting rather than shared conclusions" and says nothing of identity. The [Governance Specification](/governance/spec#the-meridian-compact) defines the Compact, a Tier 1 commitment, as "identity through practice, not through belief", and a change to the Compact's definition is Protected at Tier 2. The Amendment Log has no entry for the change, and the September audit, which read the Glossary in full, did not notice it. The current definition is not wrong about what it says. It has lost the part of the commitment that the Stability Hierarchy uses to name it, on the page a reader consults to learn what the term means.

Two Tier 1 changes were decided in September, on 2026-09-25 and 2026-09-30, and both were decided on the day they were proposed, against the [Specification's recommended window of 72 hours](/governance/spec#42-deliberation-structure). Both deviations are recorded with their reasons, and both reasons are sound: each change adopted wording the Codex had already published. Two in one week is still a pattern to watch while the Council is not yet active.

**Range Position:** Within the Meridian Range, with a lean toward Decay in the recording layer that is new this cycle. The governance architecture works when a change is presented to it as an amendment. When a change travels inside a pass presented as wording, it can reach the published text without the architecture seeing it.

**Inheritance Probes Applied:** *Rules-in-Use:* the rule as written covers every protected change; the rule as practiced covers every change someone identifies as protected, and the September passes show the difference. *Murphyjitsu:* the failure was foreseeable, because a phrasing pass over eleven chapters necessarily passes over protected definitions, and the pass had no step that asked which of its changes did. *Goodhart's Law:* a log with many careful entries can be read as evidence that nothing was missed, which is the inference the log's own opening section warns against.

### Domain 6: Relationship to Other Systems

Two September decisions changed how the Codex positions itself among other systems. The first describes the Codex as "a universal language", with the article "a" and never "the" or "the first". It is accompanied by statements that the Codex leaves the choice of which world to build to those who speak it, and that, where an alternative proves better, "the Codex yields its place to it". The Proposition still says the Codex "is not 'the framework'". This keeps the non-arbiter posture the August and September audits credited, and the first page's lineage now names its people across continents and centuries: Ibn al-Haytham in Basra and Cairo, Epictetus, Sima Qian at the court of Han China, Nikolai Vavilov in Stalin's prisons, Malala Yousafzai on a school bus in Pakistan.

The second decision, of 2026-09-27, makes religion the Codex's contrast and never its source. The [Proposition's paragraph on the Codex and religion](/codex/the-proposition#what-we-offer-and-demand) describes religions as answers to two human needs whose belonging "was bought with belief": "Their answers were placed beyond question, doubt was often punished as heresy, and their reach ended where belief ended." The [Governance chapter](/codex/the-governance#the-framework-and-governance) says that the frameworks that held longest, "the religions among them, were built to be fixed: presented as final revelation, placed beyond question". As descriptions of every religion, these sentences claim more than the record supports. Revelation, heresy and conversion by conquest describe some traditions well and others poorly. Buddhist and Jain traditions, for example, contain long practices of argument and of testing teachings against experience. The Proposition partly concedes this when it says that "for a tradition that holds all its commitments to that scrutiny, the Codex can be a complement". The sentence before that concession does not carry it.

The more consequential tension is inside the Codex's own architecture. The Inheritance's cross-tradition profiles are the Codex's main answer to the objection that it was built from one intellectual context, the objection held as the [sixth entry of the Standing Critique](/governance/standing-critique#6-the-inheritances-uneven-cross-tradition-coverage) and the [third of Open Tensions](/governance/open-tensions#3-civilizational-scope-vs-cultural-specificity). Several of those profiles say plainly that they inherit from religious lineages: [Dependent Origination](/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/dependent-origination) states that "it inherits the tool from Buddhist philosophy and practice", and [Sangha](/inheritance/belonging-through-practice/sangha), [Wise Attention](/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/wise-attention) and [Anekāntavāda](/inheritance/triangulating-across-disciplines/anekantavada-syadvada) come from Buddhist and Jain practice. The decision of 2026-09-27 says that religion "is not listed among the traditions the Codex draws on". Either the decision is read as applying to religion as an authority structure and not to practices that religious traditions developed, in which case the published sentences should say so, or it constrains what the Inheritance may admit and from where, in which case it narrows the very expansion that answers the single-context objection. The caretakers' reconciliation of 2026-09-30 noted the question at tool level and listed it among smaller corrections. This audit reads it as larger than that, because how it is answered decides whether the Codex's response to its single-context origin still stands.

**Range Position:** Within the Meridian Range, with a lean toward Control in how the Codex describes one class of other systems. It describes religions by the features of some of them, from a vantage the Codex's own Standing Critique identifies as its blind spot.

**Inheritance Probes Applied:** *Charitable Interpretation:* the strongest reading of the decision is that it targets belief placed beyond examination, which the Codex is built to be the opposite of, and not the practices traditions developed. *Two-Eyed Seeing:* a framework that draws on several lineages has to be able to say what it takes from each and what it declines, in terms each lineage would recognize. *Rectification of Names:* "religion" in the published sentences names one shape of tradition and is applied to all of them.

## Integration Through the Three Disciplines

**Through the Foundation:** honest inquiry was practiced on the Codex's largest concepts this month. The Range, the Prime Directive and the definition of Decay were each changed because the earlier wording said something the caretakers no longer believed, and each change made the Codex claim less or claim more exactly. The same discipline was applied less carefully to the Codex's smaller sentences. A phrasing pass that improved how the chapters sound also introduced "only" into a descriptive sentence, and that word asserts what the Codex's own Glossary denies. The Foundation's question, "what evidence would change my mind about this?", was asked of the Range and not, in that pass, of the sentence that grounds the Prime Directive in game theory.

**Through Reality:** seeing what is actually happening means seeing the Codex's own record for what it is. The record shows a governance architecture that catches protected changes when they are presented as amendments and misses them when they arrive inside a pass presented as wording. It shows two quotations on a live instrument that now point at sentences that no longer exist. It also shows a framework that defines one class of other systems by features that fit only some of them, while drawing tools from the ones the definition fits worst. None of these is visible from the chapters alone; each becomes visible only when the chapters, the log and the instruments are read together.

**Through the Bond:** the partnership did the cooperative work well in the places where it was asked to. The Founding Caretaker raised the objection that led to the sentence on the pulls; the AI partner raised the objection that led to a published reversal in the Standard, and recorded it in the ledger, which marks it as its least independent entry, because the AI partner raised it inside the caretakers' own work and also co-writes the record. The caretakers found their own omissions and stated them the same day. What the Bond cannot do on its own is guarantee that the checks run when no one is looking for them. That is a job for structure, which the Bond's own chapter assigns to "institutionalized dissent" and to resilience "built before the shock".

**As integrated system:** the three readings converge on one pattern. The Codex's commitments were held at full strength where a concept was the subject of the work. Its recording and precision were weaker where a sentence was only the object of a wording pass. That is the ordinary failure of a framework under heavy revision, and the remedy is ordinary as well: a step in each pass that asks whether any change touches a protected definition, a descriptive claim, or a quotation on a live instrument.

## The Compact Test

The Codex practices identity-as-practice, and this month's evidence for it is unusually direct. It changed the definition of its own central concept twice in five days. It wrote into that definition that agreement with the Codex kept by belonging is past the Codex's own edge toward Control. And it told its readers to test its pages against reality and to say where it is wrong. A framework organized around conclusions it must defend would not redefine the Range in public, and it would not concede that its own vocabulary can be spoken in Decay.

The test also reaches the Compact's own definition, and there the evidence is mixed. The Compact is defined in the Codex's highest tier as identity through practice, not belief. The Glossary entry a reader consults for the term has, since 2026-08-31, described a shared way of meeting and said nothing about identity, and no entry in the Amendment Log records the change. The practice is intact. The Codex's definition of the practice has lost its central clause without anyone deciding that it should. Restoring it, and recording why it was lost, is a test the Codex should pass easily.

## Prime Directive Connection

The Codex serves the conditions for cooperation across minds, and this month it served them more clearly than before. The Range's two edges give any community a test it can apply to itself: whether its agreements were reached by evidence and can still be revised, and whether people who differ still argue by tests both sides accept. The [Problem's new paragraph on who directs capable systems](/codex/the-problem#part-c-the-stakes) names a check on power that capable systems could remove and asks the three questions on which that check now turns: whether a system will decline openly what a soldier or a clerk would have been right to refuse, whether its builders and deployers will let it, and whether the rest of us can know which systems will.

Both open questions below bear on the directive. A governance record that misses protected changes cannot show a later mind, or a later caretaker, that the Codex's foundation was changed only by the process it describes. And a framework that offers itself as a universal language spoken across traditions needs an account of what it takes from those traditions that its readers in them would accept. Neither narrows the conditions for cooperation today. Both would, if left to stand across several cycles.

## Open Questions

September's single open question is resolved. Two new questions clear the threshold.

**1. Protected changes reach the published text inside wording passes without their Amendment Log entries, because no pass checks its changes against the Stability Hierarchy.** Three changes to protected definitions were published without entries: the Glossary's definition of the Meridian Compact on 2026-08-31, which lost the clause on identity that the Tier 1 definition is built on; the Governance chapter's statement of the disciplined practice structure on 2026-09-28; and the Glossary's definition of Decay on 2026-09-30. The last two were recorded late, the same day the caretakers found them. The first is recorded nowhere. Four further September changes are still unclassified. The caretakers have already added a close-out check for the precedent ledger after it fell behind, and the Amendment Log has no equivalent check. The question meets all four tests. It concerns the governance record that the Codex's protection against founder capture rests on before the Council activates. It is rooted in published commits. It points to bounded work: classify and record the open changes, decide whether to restore the Compact's identity clause, and add a Stability Hierarchy check to every pass that edits chapters, the Glossary or the Specification. And it carries persistence risk, because the Amendment Log's own opening says that a log missing changes "would reveal the architecture as performance", and the Codex will keep revising its chapters at this pace until the Council's activation date. The next audit checks whether the open changes have entries and whether a check now runs.

**2. The decision that religion is the Codex's contrast and never its source is in tension with the Inheritance's cross-tradition profiles and with the Codex's answer to its single-context origin.** The Proposition and the Governance chapter describe all religions by features that fit some traditions and not others: final revelation, answers placed beyond question, and conversion by conquest. Meanwhile, the Inheritance profiles that answer the Standing Critique's sixth objection draw on Buddhist, Jain and other religious lineages and say so. The question meets all four tests. It concerns the Codex's claims about other systems and the architecture of the Inheritance. It is rooted in published text on both sides. It points to a decision about whether the rule governs religion as an authority structure or the sources of individual tools, and to a calibration of the two generalizing sentences. And it carries persistence risk, because the October Inheritance Audit will review those profiles, and the outward discovery the Codex has committed to will keep meeting practices developed inside religious traditions. Naming the tension here does not resolve it. The next audit checks whether the caretakers have decided how the rule applies to tools, and whether the two sentences describe religions as the record shows them.

## Summary Diagnosis

**The Meridian Codex continues to hold the Meridian Range.** September's open question is resolved: the stakes register is calibrated on every page, and this month's test of the AI-trajectory reading found it current against the evidence of the window. The Codex redefined its central concept in public, in the direction of greater precision. It applied that concept's two edges to itself, and it corrected two of its own statements about how much criticism had changed it, both times in the direction of claiming less.

The lean this audit finds is a new one. The August and September audits found the Codex claiming more than its evidence supported. This audit finds changes to protected text that reached the published Codex without an entry in the Amendment Log. Three protected definitions changed without an entry at the time, one of them the definition of the Compact itself, and four more changes wait to be classified. The changes were made in good faith and most were improvements. What the record lacks is a step that would have caught them before publication, as a step now catches what objections from artificial minds change. The second question is narrower and deeper: whether a framework that makes religion its contrast can still draw on the practices of religious traditions, and say plainly that it does.

**In the Codex's own language:** the work this month moved the Range's edges outward on the Codex's largest concepts. On its record of its own changes, the edge toward Decay came closer, because the record now holds only what someone noticed. The remedy is a check that runs without anyone deciding to look.
