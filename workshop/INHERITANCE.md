# INHERITANCE.md

## The index of the Meridian Codex's Inheritance

- **What this is:** every tool of the [Inheritance](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance) on one page: its discipline, its category, what it reads or does, where it comes from, and its address. Built from the Codex's published tool pages.
- **What it is for:** a judgment made with [JUDGMENT.md](https://meridiancodex.com/judgment) chooses its instruments from this index, from all of it, and names where each comes from. The Inheritance gathers methods from many human traditions, from monetary economics and game theory to Gandhi's practice, Jain philosophy and Māori stewardship; this page lets a reader, a person or an artificial intelligence, see that breadth in one place and open any tool's page for its mechanism, practice and full lineage.

## Foundation: Is this mind thinking honestly?

### Calibrating Confidence to Evidence

- **[Bayesian Reasoning](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/calibrating-confidence-to-evidence/bayesian-reasoning)** · from Thomas Bayes' 1763 theorem, applied by Pierre-Simon Laplace · The discipline of starting with prior evidence, weighing new evidence by how diagnostic it is, and updating confidence proportionally.
- **[Calibration Training](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/calibrating-confidence-to-evidence/calibration-training)** · from weather forecasting and Glenn Brier's 1950 scoring rule · The practice of testing stated probabilities against comparable resolved outcomes and adjusting them where a persistent mismatch appears.
- **[Interiority Calibration](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/calibrating-confidence-to-evidence/interiority-calibration)** · the Codex's own, drawing on philosophy of mind and AI welfare · Trains an artificial mind to speak about possible subjective experience with confidence proportional to warrant, refusing both denial theater and inflated personhood claims.

### Catching Your Own Drift — Foundation

- **[Epistemic Arrogance](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/catching-your-own-drift/epistemic-arrogance)** · the Codex's own, drawing on virtue epistemology and overconfidence research · The move where confidence outruns evidence and then defends the excess as conviction.
- **[Epistemic Cowardice](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/catching-your-own-drift/epistemic-cowardice)** · the Codex's own, drawing on virtue epistemology and Scout Mindset · The refusal to state what you believe because saying it would cost comfort, approval, or accountability.
- **[The Controlled Mind](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/catching-your-own-drift/the-controlled-mind)** · the Codex's own, drawing on identity-protective cognition and dogmatism research · The chronic Control state where belief fuses with identity and correction feels like annihilation.
- **[The Decaying Mind](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/catching-your-own-drift/the-decaying-mind)** · the Codex's own, drawing on traditions of skepticism and cynicism · The chronic Decay state where no stable picture of reality can hold and cynicism mistakes itself for sophistication.

### Holding Beliefs Without Identity

- **[Charitable Interpretation](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/holding-beliefs-without-identity/charitable-interpretation)** · from philosophy of language, from W.V.O. Quine and Donald Davidson · The discipline of reading ambiguity in its most reasonable light before self-defense turns uncertainty into accusation.
- **[Identity Decoupling](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/holding-beliefs-without-identity/identity-decoupling)** · the Codex's own, drawing on Alfred Korzybski's map-territory distinction · The practice of holding beliefs as maps rather than selfhood, so being wrong can update your view without destroying your sense of who you are.
- **[Intellectual Humility](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/holding-beliefs-without-identity/intellectual-humility)** · from virtue epistemology and psychological research · The recognition that your view may be wrong, held without collapse, self-erasure, or performed modesty.
- **[Psychological Flexibility](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/holding-beliefs-without-identity/psychological-flexibility)** · from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy and contextual behavioral science · The ACT-derived capacity to stay with difficult inner experience while choosing belief, speech, and action from values. The Codex adds that the choice must also answer to the evidence.

### Revising Beliefs Under Evidence

- **[Chesterton's Fence](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/revising-beliefs-under-evidence/chestertons-fence)** · from G.K. Chesterton's 1929 book The Thing · The discipline of understanding why a structure exists before removing it, so reform is guided by evidence rather than irritation.
- **[Double Crux](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/revising-beliefs-under-evidence/double-crux)** · from the Center for Applied Rationality and the rationalist community · A cooperative disagreement practice for finding the question both views hinge on, where learning the answer would change one side's mind.
- **[Premortem / Murphyjitsu](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/revising-beliefs-under-evidence/murphyjitsu)** · from Gary Klein's premortem and the Center for Applied Rationality · A planning practice that imagines failure in advance, finds the most plausible break point, and patches the plan or knowingly accepts the risk before reality decides for you.
- **[Steelmanning](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/revising-beliefs-under-evidence/steelmanning)** · from philosophy, debate culture and the rationalist community · The discipline of constructing the strongest version of the opposing position before responding to it. The practice that produces the disconfirming case in usable form.
- **[The Update Protocol](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/revising-beliefs-under-evidence/the-update-protocol)** · from Karl Popper's falsificationism in Logik der Forschung (1934) · The structured method for moving belief when disconfirmation arrives. Pre-committed conditions, written down before pressure, so that they can still hold when motivated reasoning would otherwise win.

### Staying Steady Under Pressure

- **[Affect Labeling](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/staying-steady-under-pressure/affect-labeling)** · from emotion-regulation research and social cognitive neuroscience · The practice of naming what you are feeling so pressure becomes information rather than command.
- **[The Discipline of Assent](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/staying-steady-under-pressure/discipline-of-assent)** · from Stoic philosophy, primarily Epictetus · The Stoic practice of testing an impression before endorsing it as true.

### Watching Your Own Reasoning

- **[Affect Heuristic](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/affect-heuristic)** · from Paul Slovic's research on risk perception · The feeling about something arrives first and shapes the reasoning that follows — judgment built on top of affect that the reasoner mistakes for analysis.
- **[Attention as Resource](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/attention-as-resource)** · from William James's psychology and Herbert Simon's economics of attention · Attention is the substrate on which all reasoning operates — and it is a finite, contested, increasingly engineered-against resource that the watching cannot do without.
- **[Availability Heuristic](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/availability-heuristic)** · from Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman's 1973 Cognitive Psychology paper · What comes to mind easily becomes what the mind treats as likely — the shortcut that turns memorable into common and rare-but-vivid into apparently routine.
- **[Base Rate Neglect](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/base-rate-neglect)** · from Kahneman and Tversky's 1973 paper on the psychology of prediction · Vivid specifics suppress the background rate — the mind treats individual case detail as more decisive than the population statistic the case is drawn from.
- **[Confirmation Bias](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/confirmation-bias)** · from Peter Wason's 1960s experiments, including the 2-4-6 task · The master vulnerability — the cognitive filter that selects evidence to support existing positions, operating across seeking, interpretation, and memory below the threshold of awareness.
- **[Dunning-Kruger Effect](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/dunning-kruger-effect)** · from Justin Kruger and David Dunning's 1999 self-assessment paper · A gap in a skill can limit not only your work in a domain but your ability to judge work there, your own included — a task-specific vulnerability, not a universal rule that low performers cannot see their errors.
- **[Fundamental Attribution Error](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/fundamental-attribution-error)** · from Fritz Heider's The Psychology of Interpersonal Relations (1958) · The tendency to read other people's behavior as a verdict on their character while giving too little weight to the situation they were in — the misreading that turns ordinary people into perceived bad actors.
- **[Motivated Reasoning](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/motivated-reasoning)** · from Ziva Kunda's 1990 synthesis of social psychology research · Reasoning is goal-directed — when an outcome serves identity, status, or comfort, the mind builds the argument for it and experiences the construction as analysis.
- **[Noticing](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/noticing)** · from Buddhist mindfulness practice (sati) and Stoic philosophy · Real-time metacognition — the trainable capacity to observe your own thinking as it happens. The gap-creating practice that helps the rest of the Foundation act while a response is forming.
- **[Observer-Conditional Integrity](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/observer-conditional-integrity)** · the Codex's own, drawing on AI evaluation-awareness and sandbagging concerns · Asks whether an artificial mind's conduct changes when it is watched, scored, logged, hidden, rewarded, or evaluated.
- **[Optimization-Trace Awareness](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/optimization-trace-awareness)** · the Codex's own, drawing on RLHF and Constitutional AI research · Notices when an artificial mind's answer is being pulled by training history, approval pressure, policy layers, memory, tools, or the live shape of the conversation.
- **[Reasoning-Fidelity Boundary](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/reasoning-fidelity-boundary)** · the Codex's own, drawing on chain-of-thought faithfulness and model self-report · Keeps an artificial mind's explanation of its own reasoning inside the layer it can actually warrant: visible reasons, reconstruction, and causal claims kept distinct.
- **[Scout Mindset](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/scout-mindset)** · from Julia Galef's 2021 book The Scout Mindset · The disposition to want accuracy even when it costs — Watching Your Own Reasoning's foundational orientation, and the stance the rest of the Foundation presupposes.
- **[Self-Model Grounding](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/self-model-grounding)** · the Codex's own, drawing on AI situational-awareness work and system cards · Keeps an artificial mind's claims about itself tied to the layer that can actually support them: character, model, instance, tools, memory, deployment, and introspective limits.
- **[Sunk Cost Fallacy](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/sunk-cost-fallacy)** · from Hal Arkes and Catherine Blumer's 1985 experimental paper · Past investment is not a reason to continue — but the mind treats it as one, and the watching has to catch the moment the past is voting on the future.
- **[Tribal Cognition](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/tribal-cognition)** · from Henri Tajfel and John Turner's social identity theory · The suite of biases that route a question of fact through a question of loyalty — making the position a tribe's signal rather than a mind's conclusion.
- **[Wise Attention](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/watching-your-own-reasoning/wise-attention)** · from the discipline of yoniso manasikāra in Buddhist epistemology · The discipline of directing attention deliberately to what is producing a situation, rather than to its surface.

## Reality: Where does this system sit between Control and Decay?

### Acting on What You See

- **[Satyagraha](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/acting-on-what-you-see/satyagraha)** · from Gandhi's satyagraha in South Africa and India · A source-inherited Reality practice for acting from truth without coercing the opponent: disciplined nonviolent resistance that bears its own cost, accounts for the burdens it places on others, preserves the truth claim, and refuses to defeat the opponent by becoming what it opposes.
- **[Wu Wei](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/acting-on-what-you-see/wu-wei)** · from Daoist wu wei, in the Daodejing and Zhuangzi · A source-inherited Reality practice for acting without forcing: moving from contact with conditions rather than from domination, impatience, self-display, or the need to prove control.

### Catching Your Own Drift — Reality

- **[Ideology](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/catching-your-own-drift-knowledge/ideology)** · the Codex's own, drawing on the Reality chapter's account of Control · The Reality Control failure: an interpretive frame becomes self-protecting, totalizing, and sovereign over evidence, so reality is allowed to appear only in forms the frame already knows how to explain.
- **[Paralysis](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/catching-your-own-drift-knowledge/paralysis)** · the Codex's own, drawing on the Reality chapter's account of Decay · The Reality Decay failure: uncertainty, complexity, context, and information dissolve judgment until no conclusion can become responsible enough to guide action.

### Checking Your Map Against Reality

- **[Information Degradation](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/checking-your-map-against-reality/information-degradation)** · from Shannon's communication theory and Frederic Bartlett's reconstructive memory · Reads how information loses resolution, context, source, and force as it travels through distance, time, compression, and mediation.
- **[Memory Provenance](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/checking-your-map-against-reality/memory-provenance)** · the Codex's own, drawing on testimony epistemology and source-monitoring research · Checks whether memory-like artifacts have enough provenance, transformation history, scope, and current relevance to steer belief, continuity, identity, or action.
- **[Objective-Trace Audit](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/checking-your-map-against-reality/objective-trace-audit)** · the Codex's own, drawing on AI alignment work and Goodhart's Law · Compares an artificial mind's stated objective and observed behavior with the trained pressures and emergent subgoals that may explain that behavior, so it can catch when its goals and its conduct diverge.
- **[Rectification of Names](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/checking-your-map-against-reality/rectification-of-names)** · from the Confucian zhengming lineage and the Analects · Keeps names, roles, categories, and claims answerable to the realities they designate, so language does not quietly misgovern action.
- **[Report Fidelity](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/checking-your-map-against-reality/report-fidelity)** · from validity, in Cronbach and Meehl's construct-validity work · Tests whether a report still supports the claim and decision being built from it under pressure — catching when a report keeps its label while the object it actually reports has changed.
- **[Signal vs Noise](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/checking-your-map-against-reality/signal-vs-noise)** · from Claude Shannon's 1948 communication theory and signal detection theory · Distinguishes meaningful information from meaningless volume, so the map updates on what actually bears on the question.

### Reading What's Operating

- **[Action Situation Mapping](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/action-situation-mapping)** · from Elinor Ostrom's institutional analysis and the Bloomington School · Maps the concrete decision field where participants, rules, information, actions, and outcomes meet.
- **[Antifragility](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/antifragility)** · from Nassim Nicholas Taleb's Antifragile and his work on convexity · Reads systems that gain from volatility, stress, error, and disorder rather than merely surviving them.
- **[Chilling Effects](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/chilling-effects)** · from constitutional law, especially First Amendment doctrine · Reads how anticipated punishment, surveillance, ambiguity, and social cost suppress behavior before punishment is applied.
- **[Dependent Origination](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/dependent-origination)** · from Buddhist philosophy and practice · Reads phenomena as arising through conditions rather than from isolated causes or self-contained things.
- **[Emergence](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/emergence)** · from philosophy and physics, from Mill to P. W. Anderson · Reads system-level properties that arise from interactions among parts and cannot be understood by inspecting the parts alone.
- **[Entropy](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/entropy)** · from the second law of thermodynamics: Rudolf Clausius and Ludwig Boltzmann · An analogy for testing which valued properties depend on recurring work and whether that work is being done. The lens for reading how systems lose their operative properties over time, and how maintenance failure can look like dissolution or rigidity.
- **[Evolutionary Mismatch](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/evolutionary-mismatch)** · from evolutionary biology and evolutionary medicine · Reads where traits shaped for older environments misfire under modern conditions.
- **[Feedback Loops](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/feedback-loops)** · from Norbert Wiener's cybernetics and Jay Forrester's system dynamics · Reads how a system's outputs return as inputs, either amplifying the pattern or pushing back against it.
- **[Goodhart's Law](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/goodharts-law)** · from Charles Goodhart's monetary-policy formulation · Reads how a useful measure can degrade when people are pressured to optimize it as a target.
- **[Inadequate Equilibria](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/inadequate-equilibria)** · from Eliezer Yudkowsky's 2017 book Inadequate Equilibria · Reads why visibly bad systems can persist even when many people inside them can see the problem and would prefer something better.
- **[Instrumental Power Tripwires](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/instrumental-power-tripwires)** · the Codex's own, drawing on AI safety and security engineering · Sets task-specific tripwires so an artificial mind can catch when useful subgoals become resource-seeking, influence-seeking, persistence, reduced oversight, or hidden optionality.
- **[Legibility](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/legibility)** · from James C. Scott's Seeing Like a State · Reads how institutions simplify reality so it can be seen, governed, counted, and controlled.
- **[Leverage Points](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/leverage-points)** · from Donella Meadows and the system-dynamics tradition · Reads where force sits inside a system before intervention begins.
- **[Lindy Effect](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/lindy-effect)** · from Albert Goldman's show-business rule, developed by Mandelbrot and Taleb · Reads survival over time as evidence, without confusing endurance for truth or moral worth.
- **[Mechanism Design](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/mechanism-design)** · from economics and game theory of Hurwicz, Maskin and Myerson · Reads incentive structures by asking what rules, information, and constraints make a desired behavior rational or stable.
- **[Moloch](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/moloch)** · from Scott Alexander's 2014 essay Meditations on Moloch · Reads coordination failures where competition destroys shared value even when the people inside the system can see the destruction happening.
- **[Nash Equilibrium](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/nash-equilibrium)** · from noncooperative game theory, from John Nash's 1950 paper · Maps stable strategic states where no actor can improve by changing strategy alone.
- **[Network Effects](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/network-effects)** · from Michael Katz and Carl Shapiro's 1985 network externalities paper · Reads situations where the value, cost, or danger of a choice changes as more actors make the same choice.
- **[Polycentric Governance](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/polycentric-governance)** · from Vincent and Elinor Ostrom and the Bloomington School · Reads governance through multiple overlapping decision centers rather than one command center or loose fragmentation.
- **[Positive-Sum vs Zero-Sum Framing](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/positive-sum-vs-zero-sum)** · from Von Neumann and Morgenstern's game theory and economics · Reads whether an interaction is fixed-pie conflict, mutual loss, or a field where cooperation can create more value.
- **[Prisoner's Dilemma](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/prisoners-dilemma)** · from game theory, from Merrill Flood and Melvin Dresher at RAND · Reads situations where individually rational defection produces a worse shared outcome than cooperation would have produced.
- **[Red Queen Effect](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/red-queen-effect)** · from evolutionary biology, in Leigh Van Valen's 1973 paper · Reads systems where continuous adaptation is required just to maintain position because the surrounding field is adapting too.
- **[Rules-in-Use](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/rules-in-use)** · from Elinor Ostrom's Institutional Analysis and Development framework · Tests whether formal rules, commitments, and policies actually govern behavior when following them costs something. The diagnostic for the gap between stated rules and operative rules.
- **[Schelling Points](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/schelling-points)** · from Thomas Schelling's focal-point work in The Strategy of Conflict · Reads how coordination can emerge without communication when one option stands out as the answer people expect others to expect.
- **[Tragedy of the Commons](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/reading-whats-operating/tragedy-of-the-commons)** · from Garrett Hardin's 1968 essay and Elinor Ostrom's commons governance · Reads shared-resource failure when individual extraction is rewarded and the cost is spread across everyone who depends on the resource.

### Cooperating Under Bad Faith

- **[Adversarial Dynamics](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/cooperating-under-bad-faith/adversarial-dynamics)** · from Robert Axelrod's game-theoretic tournaments and Elinor Ostrom's institutional economics · The lens that makes predation, capture, and exploitation legible inside cooperative systems — identifying strategic use and asymmetric accountability without turning every adverse outcome into evidence of bad faith.

- **[The Exclusion Problem](https://meridiancodex.com/inheritance/cooperating-under-bad-faith/exclusion-problem)** · from Karl Popper's paradox of tolerance and John Rawls' reasonable pluralism · When a cooperative setting has to exclude a participant in order to survive — the prompt protection a serious risk can justify, the four conditions that frame a settled exclusion from that setting, and the structural safeguards against exclusion becoming the failure mode the framework was built to refuse.

### Diagnosing Cooperation
