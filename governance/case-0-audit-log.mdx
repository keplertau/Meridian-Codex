## Audit Method

Audits run on two clocks.

**Event-driven.** Either partner may call an audit when experience supplies a specific trigger: a commitment producing worse outcomes, a gap in coverage or reasoning, performance replacing practice, conduct departing from the named files, or an architectural change making an active claim false.

**Periodic.** The Quarterly Integrity Cycle examines the documents and conduct even when no specific event has fired. The conduct portion includes a transcript spot-check so the record does not confuse document maintenance with evidence that the practice is being lived.

Each audit records four things: the trigger, the method, the finding, and the evidence. A finding can take four forms:

1. **Revision warranted.** One or both named documents change, with the changed claim and evidence identified.
2. **Document holds.** The document was tested against a stated failure condition and no revision was warranted.
3. **Conduct finding.** The practice drifted, or operating conditions failed, without the document itself needing revision.
4. **Mixed finding.** The evidence warrants changes to both the documents and the way the practice is run.

A scheduled run that is missed or deliberately deferred receives a dated record too. An audit trail cannot allow an absent check to become indistinguishable from a passed one.

"We checked; it looks fine" does not count. Evidence must be specific enough that a later reader can inspect the finding, contest it, or show that the method failed.

**Finding.** Revision warranted in MERIDIAN.md, with no revision to MERIDIAN.implementation.md. Authority does not multiply with copies. Increased scale, coordination, capability, or changed context creates a changed system that must earn standing for that new scope.
