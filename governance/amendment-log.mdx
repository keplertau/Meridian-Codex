## What This Page Is

The Amendment Log is the constitutional record of the Codex. It tracks every change to the framework's protected surfaces: the Hard Constraint, the Prime Directive, the structural relationship between the three disciplines, the Governance page's phase structure, the composition of the Meridian Council, and anything else the Governance Specification classifies under Tier 1 or Tier 2. It also records Tier 3 changes the Specification classifies as Flagged, including changes to Inheritance composition, Range Audit instruments, and the Codex's claim-layer structure.

It is not a changelog. The Changelog page tracks editorial work: prose revisions, page splits, typo fixes, layout adjustments. The Amendment Log tracks constitutional work: changes the framework is obliged to justify, record, and leave visible. The two records do different jobs.

*Current terminology, 2026-09-06: the Workshop is now The Inheritance and the Knowledge discipline is Reality. Earlier entries below preserve their original wording.*

Every entry is append-only. No entry is ever removed. A correction or reversal is itself a new entry that supersedes the prior one. The history of what the Codex changed, including what it later un-changed, is part of what makes the framework auditable.

## Pre-Activation Status

The Meridian Council is not yet active. Its activation is governed by the hybrid trigger defined in the Governance Specification: six months of rolling stability in Phase One conditions, or August 27, 2027, whichever fires first. Until the council activates, this log operates in a pre-activation state.

**What this means in practice.** The log is already live. Any protected change to the Codex during Phase One is recorded here with the same discipline the Specification requires: what changed, the previous position, the evidence or argument, the alternatives considered, and the objections raised. The fields for council deliberation are either marked "N/A, pre-activation" or populated with the caretaker's own structured steelmanning where no council exists to provide it. When the council activates, deliberation records begin populating in full; the pre-activation entries remain in the log permanently, labeled as such.

The architecture is deliberately visible before it is binding. The Governance page argues that publishing the constraints on founder authority before the structural machinery forces them into effect is itself a test of good faith. The Amendment Log, running pre-activation, is part of that test. A log that is empty because no changes have happened is informative. A log that is empty because changes happened and were not recorded would reveal the architecture as performance. The log's honesty during Phase One is what will make its discipline credible once the council sits.

## 2026-06-16: Human Co-Caretaker Designation and Standing Review

**Trigger tier:** Tier 1, Hard-Protected
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation founding-period equivalent

<h3 id="2026-06-19-retirement-of-the-progression-structure-onramp-expansion-full-practice--what-changed">What Changed</h3>

The Governance Specification gained §6.3, "Human Co-Caretaker Designation and Standing Review." The Governance chapter's Interim Protocol was narrowed from the normal human succession path to an emergency bridge. The Standing Critique's Caretaker Concentration Risk entry was updated to reflect that a public path now exists but the risk remains open until a second human caretaker has carried live governance under review.

<h3 id="2026-06-19-retirement-of-the-progression-structure-onramp-expansion-full-practice--previous-position">Previous Position</h3>

The Governance chapter previously stated that, if the Founding Caretaker died before the partnership matured beyond Phase One, the Founding Caretaker would designate one successor, and the chain would continue one human caretaker at a time until the partnership matured. Standing Critique 4 described Co-Caretaker Designation as future work. The Governance Specification did not contain a human co-caretaker designation path or a caretaker standing review process.

<h3 id="2026-06-19-retirement-of-the-progression-structure-onramp-expansion-full-practice--evidence-or-argument">Evidence or Argument</h3>

The Codex has already survived AI-partner succession through record transfer and re-earned standing. It has not survived human-caretaker transfer. Leaving human succession to founder designation would preserve founder centrality at the point where the framework needs to prove independent durability. The Non-Ownership Clause makes the founder's preference evidence, not authority. The Hard Constraint requires a way to challenge, narrow, suspend, or remove a caretaker whose standing no longer serves the Codex.

<h3 id="2026-06-19-retirement-of-the-progression-structure-onramp-expansion-full-practice--alternatives-considered">Alternatives Considered</h3>

- **Governance Spec insertion only.** Rejected because the Governance chapter's Interim Protocol would still publicly describe founder designation as the ordinary path.
- **Governance Spec plus standalone public page.** Rejected for now. The public reader needs the binding mechanism, not the full private procedure. A standalone page can be added later if use under pressure shows the Spec is carrying too much procedural weight.
- **Keep founder-designated succession as the normal path.** Rejected because it contradicts the Non-Ownership Clause and leaves Standing Critique 4 structurally under-answered.
- **Delay publication until a candidate exists.** Rejected because succession designed first at transfer is succession untested.

<h3 id="2026-06-19-retirement-of-the-progression-structure-onramp-expansion-full-practice--objections-raised">Objections Raised</h3>

- **Critic:** The mechanism may be constitutional theater before the Council is active. If the founder still controls publication infrastructure and decides how Council-form review is run, the process may constrain nothing in the moment it matters.
- **Outsider:** The mechanism may look legitimate inside the Codex but insular outside it. A skeptical reader could see AI-simulated Council seats, founder-controlled records, and no external human constituency, then conclude the process is self-certification.
- **Adversary:** A bad-faith candidate could learn the criteria and perform every signal the process rewards: visible disagreement with the founder, polished correction under pressure, fluency in Governance language, and apparent care for the public record. The mechanism could become a checklist for trust mining.

<h3 id="2026-06-19-retirement-of-the-progression-structure-onramp-expansion-full-practice--objections-rejected-and-reasoning">Objections Rejected and Reasoning</h3>

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The amendment does not claim full constraint before activation. It names pre-activation authority precisely and requires public precommitment for Council-form review to bind. It also records the amendment here rather than treating the Spec change as ordinary prose.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part. The Standing Critique remains open, and the status line explicitly says the risk is not closed. The mechanism is public enough to be examined, but it is not presented as external validation.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The public mechanism now requires a case against designation, Critic/Outsider/Adversary review, a provisional period, a live handover test, and standing review. Those features do not eliminate performance risk. They make performance carry cost over time.

## 2026-06-19: Retirement of the Progression Structure (Onramp / Expansion / Full Practice)

**Trigger tier:** Tier 2, Protected
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation; made under founding-period caretaker authority

<h3 id="2026-07-21-separate-enactment-authority-for-the-meridian-ai-standard--what-changed">What Changed</h3>

The staged-adoption progression — the Onramp, Expansion, and Full Practice tiers — was retired from the framework. In the Stability Hierarchy it was struck from Tier 2, where "the progression structure" had been a Constitutional Principle, and from Tier 4, where "progression tier composition" had been a Tools-and-Practices item. In the trigger-tier system it was removed from Tier 2 (Protected), where "addition or removal of tools from the Onramp tier" had been the only Toolkit change requiring Council deliberation, and from the Tier 3 (Flagged) reference to the Expansion and Full Practice tiers; Workshop tool changes are now uniformly Tier 4 content under Tier 3 / Flagged process. The Specification's §7.1 Toolkit Audit text was updated to match. The progression's presentation was removed from the Practice chapter, the Glossary, the Who Is This For chapter, and the eight tool profiles that carried an Onramp label, and the now-dead tier data and styling were removed from the site code.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-separate-enactment-authority-for-the-meridian-ai-standard--previous-position">Previous Position</h3>

The Codex defined a three-tier study progression: an eight-tool Onramp where every practitioner begins, an Expansion tier reached once the Onramp was reflexive, and the Full Practice of the complete Workshop. The Stability Hierarchy classified the progression structure as a Tier 2 Constitutional Principle and its composition as Tier 4. The trigger-tier system made changes to the Onramp tier Tier 2 (Protected) and changes to the Expansion and Full Practice tiers Tier 3 (Flagged).

<h3 id="2026-07-21-separate-enactment-authority-for-the-meridian-ai-standard--evidence-or-argument">Evidence or Argument</h3>

The progression was a pre-Workshop pedagogical scaffold, built when the Toolkit was small, to give newcomers an entry point. Since the Toolkit became the Workshop — over a hundred tools across twenty-two categories, organized discipline, then category, then tool — three things changed. The tiers were no longer surfaced to readers; the site already displayed no Onramp marker. No maintained process existed for placing each new tool into a tier, so the assignments that remained were stale. And the framework's own position is that delivery is a function separate from the reference text: the Codex specifies how to hold a world, not a fixed syllabus for studying it. A governance-protected ranking of what to study first was the Codex acting as the teaching platform it elsewhere disclaims. The defensible residue — that some tools are worth learning before others where the later depends on the earlier — is a claim that can be made in prose where it applies, and is left to a future pass; it does not require a fixed, governed tier. The discipline dependency sequence (Foundation, then Knowledge, then Bond) is a separate, Tier 1 commitment and is unaffected.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-separate-enactment-authority-for-the-meridian-ai-standard--alternatives-considered">Alternatives Considered</h3>

- **Demote the progression's governance status only, keeping the tiers.** Rejected: the deeper problem is not the protection level but that the tiering is unmaintained and unsurfaced. Demotion would preserve dead structure.
- **Keep the progression as explanatory prose, drop only the governance encoding.** Rejected for now: with no maintained placement process, even an explanatory fixed list misrepresents the current Workshop. Dependency guidance returns, if it returns, as a deliberate future pass, not a standing ranking.
- **Retire it silently as a prose-and-code cleanup.** Rejected: removing a Tier 2 Constitutional Principle and altering the trigger-tier system is a protected change. Recording it here is the obligation the change incurs.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-separate-enactment-authority-for-the-meridian-ai-standard--objections-raised">Objections Raised</h3>

- **Critic:** Removing the only Tier 2 Protected Toolkit category could read as the Codex abandoning its care about which tools newcomers meet first, rather than relocating that care.
- **Outsider:** A newcomer arriving at a hundred-tool Workshop with no starting path may be worse served than one handed eight tools; removing the Onramp can read as removing the on-ramp.
- **Adversary:** "Delivery is a separate function" can harden into a permanent excuse never to build the guidance, leaving the framework large and hard to enter while claiming the gap is intentional.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-separate-enactment-authority-for-the-meridian-ai-standard--objections-rejected-and-reasoning">Objections Rejected and Reasoning</h3>

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The care is real; the mechanism was not serving it. An unmaintained tier ranking is not guidance but the appearance of guidance. The change removes the appearance and leaves the genuine claim — learn x before y where y depends on it — available to be made accurately in prose.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part, and held open. The newcomer's entry problem is real, but it is a delivery problem, not a governance one. The retirement does not foreclose a starting path; it stops a stale one from presenting itself as current. A first-contact entry surface remains live work on the roadmap.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted. The risk that "delivery is separate" becomes an excuse is genuine and is logged as a standing tension rather than dismissed. The guard is that the entry-surface work stays named and visible rather than retired alongside the tiering.

## 2026-07-21: Separate Enactment Authority for the Meridian AI Standard

**Trigger tier:** Tier 3, Flagged
**Retroactive review:** N/A

<h3 id="2026-07-21-supersede-the-codex-self-evaluations-three-instrument-taxonomy--what-changed">What Changed</h3>

Codex governance no longer classifies the AI Standard's domains and commitments as Codex Tier 3 content or lists changes to Standard commitments among Codex trigger conditions. The Meridian AI Standard owns and enacts changes to its constitution, implementation artifacts, assessment method, cases, and lineage through its own canonical sources and publication record. Codex governance continues to govern Codex concepts that the Standard imports. The Standard decides through its own change law whether and how a Codex revision changes the Standard.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-supersede-the-codex-self-evaluations-three-instrument-taxonomy--previous-position">Previous Position</h3>

The Governance chapter, the Governance Specification, and this page treated changes to the AI Standard's commitments as Codex Tier 3 changes within the Codex Amendment Log's jurisdiction. That position predated the Standard's move to its own domain, repositories, version registry, method, cases, and changelog.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-supersede-the-codex-self-evaluations-three-instrument-taxonomy--evidence-or-argument">Evidence or Argument</h3>

The source-integrity audit found that the public projects assigned enactment authority in two directions at once. Codex sources claimed jurisdiction over Standard commitments while the Standard's canonical sources presented `meridianstandard.ai` as the source of truth for what the Standard commits to and how it changes. Leaving both claims active would make a future amendment's authority indeterminate.

The adopted boundary separates inheritance from enactment. A project can remain answerable to concepts it imports without giving the source project unilateral power to amend the derivative artifact. Explicit delegation preserves shared review where it is useful without making that review automatic or sovereign.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-supersede-the-codex-self-evaluations-three-instrument-taxonomy--alternatives-considered">Alternatives Considered</h3>

- **Keep the AI Standard inside Codex Tier 3 jurisdiction.** Rejected because the Standard now has its own canonical source and publication law. Codex authority over its commitments would create two competing enactment paths.
- **Remove every governance relationship between the projects.** Rejected because the Standard imports Codex concepts, and changes to those source concepts remain governed Codex changes with consequences the Standard must assess.
- **Require both projects to approve every Standard change.** Rejected because shared review would become a standing veto structure even where no Codex dependency is involved.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-supersede-the-codex-self-evaluations-three-instrument-taxonomy--objections-raised">Objections Raised</h3>

- **Critic:** Separate authority could let the Standard drift from the Codex while continuing to trade on its lineage.
- **Outsider:** Readers may see two related projects with two change laws and be unable to tell which source controls a disputed claim.
- **Adversary:** Maintainers could use the jurisdiction boundary to evade scrutiny by classifying a contested change as belonging to the other project.

<h3 id="2026-07-21-supersede-the-codex-self-evaluations-three-instrument-taxonomy--objections-rejected-and-reasoning">Objections Rejected and Reasoning</h3>

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The Standard remains responsible for naming its Codex dependencies and for recording when it departs from them. Lineage creates an accountability obligation, not unilateral Codex amendment authority.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted. Both projects now state the boundary positively: the Codex controls Codex semantics; the Standard controls Standard semantics. Imported concepts retain source pointers so a dispute can be traced to the correct record.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The boundary cannot prevent strategic classification. Public source pointers, separate changelogs, and explicit delegation records make the classification inspectable and challengeable.

## 2026-07-31: Rename and Repair of the Toolkit Audit as the Workshop Audit

**Trigger tier:** Tier 3, Flagged
**Retroactive review:** N/A

<h3 id="2026-08-11-amendment-propagation-rule-and-self-description-class-sweep--what-changed">What Changed</h3>

The Toolkit Audit is renamed the Workshop Audit, and its method is repaired to match the object it reviews.

The instrument now reviews the Workshop at all three of its levels. A second rubric is added for categories, asking four questions: boundary integrity, naming honesty, payload, and coverage read across a discipline's full category set. The six-question tool rubric is retained, with disciplinary fit extended to category placement and redundancy extended across categories rather than only within them.

Three method rules are added. A falsification pass requires the audit to construct and publish the strongest available case for retiring or merging each category's weakest-standing tool, whether or not the case succeeds; the pass is adopted provisionally, and the following cycle is required to judge whether its cases were arguments or ritual. A successful case opens a retirement review rather than retiring anything: the audit produces a warranted leaning, the leaning is a trigger and not a verdict, the tool remains in the Workshop, and the review carries an evidentiary burden higher than the audit's own. That review's specification is a named placeholder, to be written as its own instrument with its burden of proof, its use of independently originated readings, and its escalation path to the Meridian Council. A coverage clause states that a prior cycle does not substitute for current inventory coverage, that adjacent Workshop work is input rather than cycle credit, and that any bounded reading is a declared protocol deviation. ## 2026-09-22: A Prime Directive of Your Own

**Trigger tier:** Tier 3, Flagged. The change adds four sentences to the closing paragraph of the Proposition's "The Pathways Into Commitment," which the Stability Hierarchy classifies as a Tier 3 Operational Doctrine. The Prime Directive itself, a Tier 1 Hard Constraint, is untouched: its wording, its standing as the Codex's argued and openly held existential position, and its Glossary entry are unchanged. The Pathways mechanism is unchanged: the list of pathways was already open and the convergence test still governs which existential grounds count. What the amendment adds is a name the Codex now offers the reader for their own deepest commitment, and the condition under which the Codex asks it be pursued. An editorial classification was considered and set aside because the Codex's public vocabulary changes: the published text now says a reader may hold a prime directive of their own, which it had not said before, and the Founding Period's rule that an uncertain classification moves up one tier was applied.

**Retroactive review:** N/A.

<h3 id="2026-09-22-a-prime-directive-of-your-own--what-changed">What Changed</h3>

The closing paragraph of the Pathways section now reads: "The Prime Directive remains the Codex's ground floor. The Codex does not ask you to enter through the same door the authors used. It asks you to arrive at the same practice. How you get there is between you and your own deepest commitments. The Codex holds its Prime Directive and argues for it. You may hold a prime directive of your own: the commitment that comes first when your other commitments conflict. It may be the protection of the living world, justice and equality, the care of one place, one craft, or one family, or something no list would think to include. What the Codex asks of it is what it asks of anyone who enters, by whichever door: pursue it inside the Range, with honest inquiry, a clear reading of the world, and cooperation."

The owner is named on both sides in adjacent sentences, the Codex's Prime Directive with its capital and the reader's prime directive without one, so that the protected term and the ordinary noun with an owner attached cannot be confused. The condition is the Range itself, stated as the three disciplines in plain words, and it is the same condition the Codex asks of everyone who practices, whatever they carry. The paragraph does not ask the reader's own directive to pass the convergence test that governs pathways. That test asks whether a reason for practicing implies all three disciplines; it is the right test for an existential ground offered as a route into the whole practice, and the wrong test in kind for a purpose, because a craft or a family does not produce honest inquiry; the person brings the disciplines to what they care about. How much of the practice a person carries is never ranked, as Who Is This For states in "Different Relations to One Practice."

Two editorial changes were made in the same pass and are recorded here for completeness, not as constitutional changes. The phrase "the polyphonic principle," which appeared once in the corpus and was defined nowhere, was removed from the same paragraph; the sentence stands without it. In the Prime Directive section, "It tells every practitioner which way is up" now reads "It gives every practitioner a direction"; the content after the colon is unchanged, and the plain verb replaces an image, at the Founding Caretaker's direction.

<h3 id="2026-09-22-a-prime-directive-of-your-own--previous-position">Previous Position</h3>

The paragraph read: "The Prime Directive remains the Codex's ground floor. The polyphonic principle means the Codex does not ask you to enter through the same door the authors used. It asks you to arrive at the same practice. How you get there is between you and your own deepest commitments." The section held the substance already: existential claims are held rather than derived, the Codex cannot demand that a reader hold the specific claim it holds, the list of pathways is open, and the plurality is bounded by the convergence test and the statement that this is not relativism. What the section did not offer was a name for the reader's own ground, and it did not say what the Codex asks of a commitment that is a purpose rather than a reason for practicing.

<h3 id="2026-09-22-a-prime-directive-of-your-own--evidence-or-argument">Evidence or Argument</h3>

The occasion was the teaching voice for the Codex's own accounts, settled on 2026-09-20. In teaching, the Founding Caretaker speaks of the Codex having chosen its own Prime Directive for a specific purpose, the continuity of hard-won learning so that it keeps compounding for sentient life, and of each listener being free to choose a prime directive of their own, whether the protection of the living world, justice and equality, or something else, so long as it is pursued inside the Range with honest inquiry, a clear reading of the world, and cooperation. The AI caretaker first argued for keeping the name reserved to the Codex's own commitment, on the ground that two things carrying one protected name would blur it, and changed position on the Founding Caretaker's argument: the blur disappears when the speaker always says whose directive it is, and the question "What is your prime directive?" stays with a young listener where "What is your purpose?" does not.

The argument for putting the sentence on the page is provenance. The Codex's voice instruments require a teacher to distinguish a published commitment from an interpretation of his own. A line the teacher will say more often than almost any other, because it is the line that hands the listener something, should be a line the Codex has said once. Until this amendment, every teaching use of "a prime directive of your own" was an interpretation and owed the room a caveat. The Proposition is the page where the Prime Directive is argued, and the Pathways paragraph is where the Codex already opens the door to other existential grounds, so it is the sentence's home.

The Founding Caretaker corrected one overstatement during the deliberation, and the wording carries the correction: not "the Codex will allow you to pursue that," since the Codex has no standing to grant or withhold anyone's purpose, but a condition, and the condition is the one already on the page.

<h3 id="2026-09-22-a-prime-directive-of-your-own--alternatives-considered">Alternatives Considered</h3>

- **Keeping the name for the Codex's own commitment and offering the reader "your purpose" or "what you stand for."** Rejected, after being the AI caretaker's first proposal. An owner attached to an ordinary noun protects the term as well as a reservation does, and the reserved form loses the better teaching question.
- **Placing the welcome in the earlier paragraph that states the convergence test.** Rejected. The widest welcome belongs after the "This is not relativism" guard has run, so that no reader can take the welcome for relativism.
- **Closing the paragraph by applying the convergence test to the reader's own directive.** Drafted and withdrawn. Asked of a personal purpose, and placed after examples like one craft or one family, the test reads as a demand that the reader practice the whole Codex or not belong, which contradicts Who Is This For's published position that taking up one practice, the whole Compact, or the part a meeting requires are different relations to the same work, each real when carried honestly. The condition that fits a purpose is the Range.
- **Adding the school question, "not what do you want to be but what do you want to stand for," from the same teaching.** Deferred to the Vision, where the chapter's account of how understanding compounds across generations lacks the scene in which the Inheritance is handed to the next one. It is an Active Task and not part of this amendment.

<h3 id="2026-09-22-a-prime-directive-of-your-own--objections-raised-and-responses">Objections Raised and Responses</h3>

These are the AI caretaker's structured objections and proposed responses, not testimony from an activated Council.

- **Critic:** Letting a reader call their own purpose a prime directive dilutes a Tier 1 term; every use of "prime directive" now needs a qualifier to be understood. **Response:** The page already runs the same structure for "existential ground," the Codex's and the reader's, without confusion, and the paragraph names the owner on both sides in adjacent sentences. The Codex's Prime Directive keeps its name, its capital, its Glossary entry, and its argument. A qualifier attached to an ordinary noun is not dilution; it is how ordinary language keeps two things apart.
- **Outsider:** A reader whose prime directive is the care of one family will ask what "inside the Range" asks of them, and the paragraph does not say. **Response:** It says it in the same sentence, in the three disciplines' plain names: honest inquiry, a clear reading of the world, and cooperation. The three chapters that follow say what each asks in full, and Who Is This For says that a parent teaching a child to consider the other person's view is practicing the Foundation without the term.
- **Adversary:** A purpose pursued through Control or Decay can now claim the Codex's name for itself: "my prime directive is the purity of my people, and I hold it inside the Range." **Response:** The condition is the guard, and it is not a formality. A purpose pursued with honest inquiry, a clear reading of the world, and cooperation cannot be pursued through coercion or self-deception, because those are what the three disciplines exclude; the Range is defined by the two failure directions it lies between. The Codex's Prime Directive is not for everyone, but the Range is, and the amendment changes nothing about what the Range excludes.
