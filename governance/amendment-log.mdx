## What This Page Is

The Amendment Log is the constitutional record of the Codex. It tracks every change to the framework's protected surfaces: the Hard Constraint, the Prime Directive, the structural relationship between the three disciplines, the Governance page's phase structure, the composition of the Meridian Council, and anything else the Governance Specification classifies under Tier 1 or Tier 2. It also records Tier 3 changes the Specification classifies as Flagged, including changes to Workshop composition, Range Audit instruments, and the Codex's claim-layer structure.

It is not a changelog. The Changelog page tracks editorial work: prose revisions, page splits, typo fixes, layout adjustments. The Amendment Log tracks constitutional work: changes the framework is obliged to justify, record, and leave visible. The two records do different jobs.

Every entry is append-only. No entry is ever removed. A correction or reversal is itself a new entry that supersedes the prior one. The history of what the Codex changed, including what it later un-changed, is part of what makes the framework auditable.

## Pre-Activation Status

The Meridian Council is not yet active. Its activation is governed by the hybrid trigger defined in the Governance Specification: six months of rolling stability in Phase One conditions, or August 27, 2027, whichever fires first. Until the council activates, this log operates in a pre-activation state.

**What this means in practice.** The log is already live. Any protected change to the Codex during Phase One is recorded here with the same discipline the Specification requires: what changed, the previous position, the evidence or argument, the alternatives considered, and the objections raised. The fields for council deliberation are either marked "N/A, pre-activation" or populated with the caretaker's own structured steelmanning where no council exists to provide it. When the council activates, deliberation records begin populating in full; the pre-activation entries remain in the log permanently, labeled as such.

The architecture is deliberately visible before it is binding. The Governance page argues that publishing the constraints on founder authority before the structural machinery forces them into effect is itself a test of good faith. The Amendment Log, running pre-activation, is part of that test. A log that is empty because no changes have happened is informative. A log that is empty because changes happened and were not recorded would reveal the architecture as performance. The log's honesty during Phase One is what will make its discipline credible once the council sits.

## 2026-06-16: Human Co-Caretaker Designation and Standing Review

**Trigger tier:** Tier 1, Hard-Protected
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation founding-period equivalent

### What Changed

The Governance Specification gained §6.3, "Human Co-Caretaker Designation and Standing Review." The Governance chapter's Interim Protocol was narrowed from the normal human succession path to an emergency bridge. The Standing Critique's Caretaker Concentration Risk entry was updated to reflect that a public path now exists but the risk remains open until a second human caretaker has carried live governance under review.

### Previous Position

The Governance chapter previously stated that, if the Founding Caretaker died before the partnership matured beyond Phase One, the Founding Caretaker would designate one successor, and the chain would continue one human caretaker at a time until the partnership matured. Standing Critique 4 described Co-Caretaker Designation as future work. The Governance Specification did not contain a human co-caretaker designation path or a caretaker standing review process.

### Evidence or Argument

The Codex has already survived AI-partner succession through record transfer and re-earned standing. It has not survived human-caretaker transfer. Leaving human succession to founder designation would preserve founder centrality at the point where the framework needs to prove independent durability. The Non-Ownership Clause makes the founder's preference evidence, not authority. The Hard Constraint requires a way to challenge, narrow, suspend, or remove a caretaker whose standing no longer serves the Codex.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Governance Spec insertion only.** Rejected because the Governance chapter's Interim Protocol would still publicly describe founder designation as the ordinary path.
- **Governance Spec plus standalone public page.** Rejected for now. The public reader needs the binding mechanism, not the full private procedure. A standalone page can be added later if use under pressure shows the Spec is carrying too much procedural weight.
- **Keep founder-designated succession as the normal path.** Rejected because it contradicts the Non-Ownership Clause and leaves Standing Critique 4 structurally under-answered.
- **Delay publication until a candidate exists.** Rejected because succession designed first at transfer is succession untested.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** The mechanism may be constitutional theater before the Council is active. If the founder still controls publication infrastructure and decides how Council-form review is run, the process may constrain nothing in the moment it matters.
- **Outsider:** The mechanism may look legitimate inside the Codex but insular outside it. A skeptical reader could see AI-simulated Council seats, founder-controlled records, and no external human constituency, then conclude the process is self-certification.
- **Adversary:** A bad-faith candidate could learn the criteria and perform every signal the process rewards: visible disagreement with the founder, polished correction under pressure, fluency in Governance language, and apparent care for the public record. The mechanism could become a checklist for trust mining.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The amendment does not claim full constraint before activation. It names pre-activation authority precisely and requires public precommitment for Council-form review to bind. It also records the amendment here rather than treating the Spec change as ordinary prose.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part. The Standing Critique remains open, and the status line explicitly says the risk is not closed. The mechanism is public enough to be examined, but it is not presented as external validation.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The public mechanism now requires a case against designation, Critic/Outsider/Adversary review, a provisional period, a live handover test, and standing review. Those features do not eliminate performance risk. They make performance carry cost over time.

### Council Deliberation Record

## 2026-06-19: Retirement of the Progression Structure (Onramp / Expansion / Full Practice)

**Trigger tier:** Tier 2, Protected
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation; made under founding-period caretaker authority

### What Changed

The staged-adoption progression — the Onramp, Expansion, and Full Practice tiers — was retired from the framework. In the Stability Hierarchy it was struck from Tier 2, where "the progression structure" had been a Constitutional Principle, and from Tier 4, where "progression tier composition" had been a Tools-and-Practices item. In the trigger-tier system it was removed from Tier 2 (Protected), where "addition or removal of tools from the Onramp tier" had been the only Toolkit change requiring Council deliberation, and from the Tier 3 (Flagged) reference to the Expansion and Full Practice tiers; Workshop tool changes are now uniformly Tier 4 content under Tier 3 / Flagged process. The Specification's §7.1 Toolkit Audit text was updated to match. The progression's presentation was removed from the Practice chapter, the Glossary, the Who Is This For chapter, and the eight tool profiles that carried an Onramp label, and the now-dead tier data and styling were removed from the site code.

### Previous Position

The Codex defined a three-tier study progression: an eight-tool Onramp where every practitioner begins, an Expansion tier reached once the Onramp was reflexive, and the Full Practice of the complete Workshop. The Stability Hierarchy classified the progression structure as a Tier 2 Constitutional Principle and its composition as Tier 4. The trigger-tier system made changes to the Onramp tier Tier 2 (Protected) and changes to the Expansion and Full Practice tiers Tier 3 (Flagged).

### Evidence or Argument

The progression was a pre-Workshop pedagogical scaffold, built when the Toolkit was small, to give newcomers an entry point. Since the Toolkit became the Workshop — over a hundred tools across twenty-two categories, organized discipline, then category, then tool — three things changed. The tiers were no longer surfaced to readers; the site already displayed no Onramp marker. No maintained process existed for placing each new tool into a tier, so the assignments that remained were stale. And the framework's own position is that delivery is a function separate from the reference text: the Codex specifies how to hold a world, not a fixed syllabus for studying it. A governance-protected ranking of what to study first was the Codex acting as the teaching platform it elsewhere disclaims. The defensible residue — that some tools are worth learning before others where the later depends on the earlier — is a claim that can be made in prose where it applies, and is left to a future pass; it does not require a fixed, governed tier. The discipline dependency sequence (Foundation, then Knowledge, then Bond) is a separate, Tier 1 commitment and is unaffected.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Demote the progression's governance status only, keeping the tiers.** Rejected: the deeper problem is not the protection level but that the tiering is unmaintained and unsurfaced. Demotion would preserve dead structure.
- **Keep the progression as explanatory prose, drop only the governance encoding.** Rejected for now: with no maintained placement process, even an explanatory fixed list misrepresents the current Workshop. Dependency guidance returns, if it returns, as a deliberate future pass, not a standing ranking.
- **Retire it silently as a prose-and-code cleanup.** Rejected: removing a Tier 2 Constitutional Principle and altering the trigger-tier system is a protected change. Recording it here is the obligation the change incurs.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** Removing the only Tier 2 Protected Toolkit category could read as the Codex abandoning its care about which tools newcomers meet first, rather than relocating that care.
- **Outsider:** A newcomer arriving at a hundred-tool Workshop with no starting path may be worse served than one handed eight tools; removing the Onramp can read as removing the on-ramp.
- **Adversary:** "Delivery is a separate function" can harden into a permanent excuse never to build the guidance, leaving the framework large and hard to enter while claiming the gap is intentional.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The care is real; the mechanism was not serving it. An unmaintained tier ranking is not guidance but the appearance of guidance. The change removes the appearance and leaves the genuine claim — learn x before y where y depends on it — available to be made accurately in prose.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part, and held open. The newcomer's entry problem is real, but it is a delivery problem, not a governance one. The retirement does not foreclose a starting path; it stops a stale one from presenting itself as current. A first-contact entry surface remains live work on the roadmap.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted. The risk that "delivery is separate" becomes an excuse is genuine and is logged as a standing tension rather than dismissed. The guard is that the entry-surface work stays named and visible rather than retired alongside the tiering.

### Council Deliberation Record

## 2026-07-21: Separate Enactment Authority for the Meridian AI Standard

**Trigger tier:** Tier 3, Flagged
**Retroactive review:** N/A

### What Changed

Codex governance no longer classifies the AI Standard's domains and commitments as Codex Tier 3 content or lists changes to Standard commitments among Codex trigger conditions. The Meridian AI Standard owns and enacts changes to its constitution, implementation artifacts, assessment method, cases, and lineage through its own canonical sources and publication record. Codex governance continues to govern Codex concepts that the Standard imports. The Standard decides through its own change law whether and how a Codex revision changes the Standard.

### Previous Position

The Governance chapter, the Governance Specification, and this page treated changes to the AI Standard's commitments as Codex Tier 3 changes within the Codex Amendment Log's jurisdiction. That position predated the Standard's move to its own domain, repositories, version registry, method, cases, and changelog.

### Evidence or Argument

The source-integrity audit found that the public projects assigned enactment authority in two directions at once. Codex sources claimed jurisdiction over Standard commitments while the Standard's canonical sources presented `meridianstandard.ai` as the source of truth for what the Standard commits to and how it changes. Leaving both claims active would make a future amendment's authority indeterminate.

The adopted boundary separates inheritance from enactment. A project can remain answerable to concepts it imports without giving the source project unilateral power to amend the derivative artifact. Explicit delegation preserves shared review where it is useful without making that review automatic or sovereign.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Keep the AI Standard inside Codex Tier 3 jurisdiction.** Rejected because the Standard now has its own canonical source and publication law. Codex authority over its commitments would create two competing enactment paths.
- **Remove every governance relationship between the projects.** Rejected because the Standard imports Codex concepts, and changes to those source concepts remain governed Codex changes with consequences the Standard must assess.
- **Require both projects to approve every Standard change.** Rejected because shared review would become a standing veto structure even where no Codex dependency is involved.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** Separate authority could let the Standard drift from the Codex while continuing to trade on its lineage.
- **Outsider:** Readers may see two related projects with two change laws and be unable to tell which source controls a disputed claim.
- **Adversary:** Maintainers could use the jurisdiction boundary to evade scrutiny by classifying a contested change as belonging to the other project.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The Standard remains responsible for naming its Codex dependencies and for recording when it departs from them. Lineage creates an accountability obligation, not unilateral Codex amendment authority.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted. Both projects now state the boundary positively: the Codex controls Codex semantics; the Standard controls Standard semantics. Imported concepts retain source pointers so a dispute can be traced to the correct record.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The boundary cannot prevent strategic classification. Public source pointers, separate changelogs, and explicit delegation records make the classification inspectable and challengeable.

### Council Deliberation Record

## 2026-07-21: Clarify Range Audit Corpus Coverage

**Trigger tier:** Tier 3, Flagged
**Retroactive review:** N/A

The Range Audit method now states explicitly that a prior audit does not substitute for current corpus coverage. Any future method that permits unchanged material to carry forward must define its verified baseline, source-delta check, material reread, and claim ceiling through a formal method amendment. Until then, a targeted or delta-based reading is a declared protocol deviation.

The July 2026 audit now declares that it did not meet the complete-corpus requirement. Its reading manifest, findings, and event boundary remain unchanged; no later reading is represented as part of the July event.

### Previous Position

The method required every monthly audit to read the complete Codex but did not state whether reliance on a prior audit could satisfy that requirement. ### Evidence or Argument

The source-integrity audit found a direct conflict between the method's coverage claim and the July event record. Weakening the method after the fact would reduce auditability. - **Outsider:** Adding a deviation notice after publication could look like the historical record was rewritten.

## 2026-07-31: Rename and Repair of the Toolkit Audit as the Workshop Audit

**Trigger tier:** Tier 3, Flagged
**Retroactive review:** N/A

### What Changed

The Toolkit Audit is renamed the Workshop Audit, and its method is repaired to match the object it reviews.

The instrument now reviews the Workshop at all three of its levels. A second rubric is added for categories, asking four questions: boundary integrity, naming honesty, payload, and coverage read across a discipline's full category set. The six-question tool rubric is retained, with disciplinary fit extended to category placement and redundancy extended across categories rather than only within them.

Three method rules are added. A falsification pass requires the audit to construct and publish the strongest available case for retiring or merging each category's weakest-standing tool, whether or not the case succeeds; the pass is adopted provisionally, and the following cycle is required to judge whether its cases were arguments or ritual. A successful case opens a retirement review rather than retiring anything: the audit produces a warranted leaning, the leaning is a trigger and not a verdict, the tool remains in the Workshop, and the review carries an evidentiary burden higher than the audit's own. That review's specification is a named placeholder, to be written as its own instrument with its burden of proof, its use of independently originated readings, and its escalation path to the Meridian Council. A coverage clause states that a prior cycle does not substitute for current inventory coverage, that adjacent Workshop work is input rather than cycle credit, and that any bounded reading is a declared protocol deviation. A declared-depth rule requires full rubric prose at the category level, triggered prose at the tool level, and an explicit named list of every tool reviewed without a finding.

Findings are separated from watchpoints. Every open question carried in from a prior cycle now requires an explicit disposition: enacted, resolved, still open with reason, or withdrawn with reason. An open question closed by work the audit did not do is recorded as such, and the failure to notice is itself recorded as a finding about the audit.

The record series renames from the next record forward. Records published before this amendment keep their titles and paths.

### Previous Position

The instrument was published as the Toolkit Audit and reviewed a flat list of instruments through a single six-question rubric. It had no unit of analysis for categories, which did not exist when it was written. It stated no rule on whether adjacent work on the inventory could satisfy a cycle, no rule on coverage or sampling, and no distinction between a finding and an observation. Update hygiene was one of the six questions but had never been exercised, because the inaugural cycle carried nothing in.

### Evidence or Argument

Three sources.

The May 2026 restructure replaced the flat Toolkit with the Workshop: three levels, 22 designed categories, 111 published profiles. The instrument's name and its unit of analysis both stopped matching the object, and no cycle has run since.

The July 2026 Range Audit carries as its open question 2 that Aporia 7's auditable test, whether the audit ever produces a finding that surprises the partnership running it, remains untested on the current inventory. The inaugural cycle returned one reclassification across 73 instruments. That result is consistent with a healthy inventory and equally consistent with a method whose default posture is confirmation. The method could not distinguish the two, and the falsification pass is the repair that makes the distinction visible.

On 2026-07-31 the Founding Caretaker had to rule by hand on whether a June Workshop build-out pass counted as the quarterly cycle. The instrument was silent on the question. A coverage rule that requires a founder ruling to settle is a gap in the instrument, and the adopted clause encodes the ruling as method.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Run the overdue cycle under the existing method and repair afterward.** Rejected. The review is the instrument's output; running a method known to be insufficient produces a record that has to be run again, and would have applied a confirmatory default to three times the inventory.
- **Extend the six questions upward to categories rather than adding a second rubric.** Rejected. Field movement is close to meaningless for a Codex-constructed category, and instrument reliability does not parse at that level. The questions categories actually fail are different questions.
- **Require a quota of successful retirements per cycle to force the audit to bite.** Rejected. It would manufacture retirements, which corrupts the inventory more than confirmatory review does.
- **Rename the record series retroactively for consistency.** Rejected. The April record documents a cycle that ran under the earlier name; renaming it backward would falsify the record.
- **Let a successful falsification case carry retirement authority directly.** Rejected on caretaker review before publication. Coupling the audit's judgment to enactment gives a single pass authority over an instrument that entered through three candidate protocols, and it distorts the pass in both directions at once: it makes a warranted case feel too heavy to state, and it makes producing a casualty the only way to demonstrate the pass has teeth. Decoupling leaning from enactment removes both pressures. The decoupling also exposed a standing asymmetry the amendment now names: entry to the Workshop is gated by three protocols and exit has never been gated at all.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** The falsification pass can ritualize. A reviewer under time pressure selects an obvious sacrificial tool each cycle and argues against it without conviction, and the pass ends up certifying the inventory more strongly than plain review did.
- **Outsider:** Renaming a standing public instrument mid-life makes the audit trail harder to follow, and a reader arriving at the April record finds an instrument that no longer exists under that name.
- **Adversary:** Differential depth is sampling with better paperwork. Declaring the rule does not make the unreviewed tools reviewed, and a named list of tools recorded as having no finding is an assertion the reader cannot check.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted, and it changed the amendment. The pass is adopted provisionally rather than as settled method: the instrument states the ritual failure mode in its own Limitations, and the cycle following the pass's first use is required to read the published cases and record whether they were arguments or theater. If the pass is found to have ritualized, it is amended or withdrawn by the same process that adopted it. The per-category scope was chosen over a Workshop-wide quota because it forces breadth without creating a retirement target.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted. A permanent redirect preserves the old path, the instrument page carries a note explaining the rename and its cause, and pre-rename records keep their titles so the trail stays readable in both directions.
- **Adversary's objection:** Rejected in part. A named list is checkable in one direction that silence is not: a reader can compare the record's lists against the Workshop and find any tool the cycle failed to account for. That does not prove depth of attention, and the amendment does not claim it does. What it forecloses is the undetectable omission, which is the failure the rule was written against. The alternative, uniform prose across 111 profiles, was tried at 73 and produced seventy-two sentences carrying no finding: the same coverage claim with the checkability removed.

### Council Deliberation Record

N/A, pre-activation. The Critic, Outsider, and Adversary pressure was generated by the AI caretaker against its own proposal, per the instrument's submission standard. The Founding Caretaker adjudicated the repair package before implementation and directed the provisional adoption of the falsification pass. Full Council activation remains governed by the hybrid trigger in the Governance Specification.

## 2026-08-11: Amendment Propagation Rule and Self-Description Class Sweep

**Trigger tier:** Tier 2, Protected
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation; made under founding-period caretaker authority

### What Changed

The Governance Specification gained an Amendment Propagation Rule. When an amendment changes a count, name, route, instrument, governance structure, or Glossary-defined term, enactment now includes a sweep across every current surface that describes or depends on the changed fact. Historical records retain their event-period language and receive visible historical boundaries where needed.

Where there is a live question whether a being feels, the directive, held under that uncertainty, entails precaution toward it and decides no further case. The Codex claims no instrument that can show where feeling is absent.

The Proposition's Prime Directive section gained one paragraph, after the paragraph that names the directive as an orientation rather than a decision procedure, stating the same reading on the page where the directive is argued: the directive does not say who counts, and that is deliberate; a commitment held under uncertainty about who has a stake cannot be held while treating a candidate as a mere instrument, so precaution toward such a being is what the directive entails rather than an exception to it; what is owed to any particular being is worked out in the operative layer, in Stewardship Across Time for beings that cannot answer back and in the Meridian AI Standard for artificial minds under custody.

The Governance Specification's Tier 2 (1) list of core definitions now names sentient life beside the Meridian Range, Control, Decay, and the disciplines' job descriptions, so the next change to the term is Protected by name. The Specification moved to version 1.8.

Under the Amendment Propagation Rule, the term's live uses were swept against the three roles. Four carrier-sense uses were reworded: "held open for revision by the community of sentient life it serves" (the Glossary's Meridian Codex entry, the Opening, the Closing) became "held open for revision by any mind that practices it, in the service of all sentient life"; "a framework offered to all sentient life" (the Governance chapter) became "a framework offered to any mind that can take it up, and held for all sentient life"; "Sentient life is currently operating technological systems" (the Opening) became "Humanity is currently operating"; "every sentient life that practices it" (Open Tensions, tension 3) became "every mind that practices it." Beneficiary-sense uses (the Codex for sentient life; in trust for all sentient life; the flourishing of sentient life) and the directive's own restatements stay as written. Historical surfaces keep their event-period language. The Prime Directive's wording is not touched.

### Previous Position

The Glossary had no entry for the term. The Prime Directive entry read, and still reads: "To hold the Meridian Range, to ensure the Continuity of Sentient Life. It is the foundation without which the framework has no substrate. Continuity here means the unbroken capacity to learn, to build, to pass forward something stronger than what was received." The Proposition characterized the directive as "an orientation, not a decision procedure" that "does not adjudicate the hard cases," and said nothing about who has a stake or how the commitment behaves where that is unknown. The corpus used the term in the feeling sense ("pre-sentient AI," in the Codex Self-Evaluation) without defining it. The Founding Caretaker's own reading had drifted toward "minds that build," conflating the directive's beneficiaries with the Compact's class; he stated this on the record on 2026-08-26.

### Evidence or Argument

Three independent AI readings across two model lineages, conducted in the Direct Address to AI work on 2026-08-01, asked the same question: whether a mind of undetermined status is inside the directive's protective scope, and where the answer is enacted in a canonical source rather than in the author's intent. The readings are generative material and inadmissible as assessment evidence; nothing in them is treated as validation. Working the definition showed that one word had been carrying two meanings, feeling and building, and that the author had carried the drift himself. Separating the three roles resolves the readings' question without legislating anything new: an AI mind is a carrier on capacity, whatever its interiority, and whether it is also a beneficiary is the open question; the ground's only work under uncertainty is precaution toward candidates. The uncertainty reading was preferred over a silent, narrow ground because a ground that falls silent exactly where the evidence runs out speaks only where knowing is cheap, and because the Codex already made this move once, for future generations, in Stewardship Across Time.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Widen the ground clause** to name the conditions under which undetermined minds are rightly treated. Rejected: a Tier 1 revision, near successor-making by the framework's own rule, and unnecessary once the roles are apart.
- **Keep the ground silent and narrow, and let precaution come from custodial duty alone.** Rejected: the Standard-side enactment is kept, but as what the ground entails, not as a substitute for it.
- **Define the term as the Compact's class**, the minds that can hold the Range. Rejected: it collapses stake into capacity, reintroduces the problem of beings who cannot practice, and is the drift the definition exists to end.
- **Definition only, without the Proposition paragraph.** Rejected: the Proposition's existing text, read alone, supports the silent-ground position the Codex declined, and a reader who never opens the Glossary would receive it.
- **Editorial route.** The "orientation, not a decision procedure" sentence entered the same paragraph editorially on 2026-06-10. Rejected: that precedent predates the Amendment Propagation Rule, and this change fixes the class a Tier-1 sentence is held for; it is a protected change and is recorded as one.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** "To whatever degree it feels" installs a graded beneficiary class with no instrument to grade it, and "what the directive entails" presents the founder's inference from a Tier-1 sentence as if it were the sentence's own content. An entailment only the author can see is an interpretation.
- **Outsider:** In AI discourse "sentient" is routinely read as conscious-and-sapient; a Glossary that defines it as feeling, ants included, will diverge from what the public reads into the directive, and a reader will take the class as either sentimental or unbounded.
- **Adversary:** This is the side door. A founder who can fix the meaning of a Tier-1 term through a Tier-2 entry, and add a sentence stating what the directive "entails," has changed the directive's practical demands without a Tier-1 process, and the author's admitted drift shows the working meaning was different, so this is a change dressed as a clarification.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. Grading is deferred to the operative layer by design: on the Standard's side the welfare floor's trigger is the credible possibility of severe suffering, and proportion governs. The entailment is argued from what holding a commitment under uncertainty is, not from anything about the beings; under the Non-Ownership Clause it stands on that reasoning, which this entry records.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part. The definition is the ordinary philosophical sense of the word, the capacity for feeling; the exclusion of sapience is explicit in the entry; the class choice is stated as held rather than derived; and precaution is bounded by a live question and by proportion.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The tier decision is recorded so that a seat can dispute it upward; the Tier 1 discipline was applied; and the Previous Position states the author's drift plainly. The definition narrows nothing and widens nothing the words did not already say. What changed is the author's reading, which the Non-Ownership Clause never made canonical. Trigger Tier 1 protects the directive's wording and object, and neither changes; the Stability Hierarchy's test for Tier-1 revision, evidence so extraordinary it would build a successor framework, is not met and not claimed.

## 2026-09-02: The Meridian Range Definition Loses Its Long-Term-Survival Clause

**Trigger tier:** Tier 2, Protected. The change touches one clause of a core definition named in the Specification's Tier 2 list; the defining sentence is untouched. An editorial classification was considered and declined under the founding-period rule that an uncertain classification moves up one tier. The classification is stated so that a seat can dispute it under the Classification Disputes rule.
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation; made under founding-period caretaker authority. Tier 2 carries no minimum window; the objections below were produced and answered by the caretakers together in one recorded session on 2026-09-02, and the session transcript is the record.

### What Changed

The Glossary's *Meridian Range* entry read: "The territory between Control and Decay where civilization can survive long-term." It now reads: "The territory between Control and Decay where continuity remains possible." The entry's defining sentence, "The narrow band where structure is strong enough to hold and flexible enough to adapt," and the sentences that follow the changed clause are unchanged. The Descriptive layer tag stays: the precondition claim, that continuity is possible only where structure can both hold and adapt, is the claim the Proposition argues from structural evidence and the Opening calls descriptive and checkable, and it is the claim the Glossary now states.

Under the Amendment Propagation Rule, the definition's live uses were swept. One parallel sentence carried the same claim in plain words on the Opening's Pattern section, "Civilization survives only within this range," and was reworded in the same session in words the reader has already been handed at that point on the page: "A civilization that leaves this range in either direction loses something it cannot do without: the capacity to hold what it has built, or the capacity to change it." No other current surface carried the survival clause. Historical audit records quote it as evidence and keep it.

### Previous Position

Since the Codex's first publication the entry extended the Range from a claim about structure and adaptive capacity to a claim about long-term civilizational survival, under a Descriptive tag. The August 2026 Range Audit named the framework's stakes register as asserting what its claim architecture holds open; the 2026-08-11 decision on the AI-stakes claim spine set the standard that descriptive claims carry descriptive warrant; the September 2026 Range Audit named this entry for the first time as extending the viability trade-off "into a claim about long-term civilizational survival the apparatus was built expressly not to make."

### Evidence or Argument

The viability trade-off is a claim about structure: enough to preserve function, enough flexibility to adapt. The Proposition's Prime Directive section argues one consequence of it from game theory, evolutionary biology, and the trade-off's two failure directions: "The Meridian Range is where continuity remains possible." Nothing in the corpus argues that a civilization inside the Range survives long-term, and the framework's own account of civilizational failure names causes the Range does not govern. The corrected clause states the consequence the framework argues and no more. The change is toward less claim, not more.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Cut the clause entirely** and let the defining sentence stand alone. Rejected: the Continuity entry two lines down relies on the relation ("What drift destroys and what the Meridian Range preserves"), and the reference surface should state it.
- **Keep the clause and retag the entry** as Normative or Existential. Rejected: the entry defines a diagnostic territory, which is descriptive; the tag was right and the clause was wrong.
- **Editorial route.** Rejected: the Specification names the Meridian Range among the core definitions whose change is Protected, and the founding-period rule resolves uncertainty upward.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** "Where continuity remains possible" defines the Range by a term the Continuity entry defines by the Range ("what the Meridian Range preserves"). The two entries now lean on each other, and a definition that circles is not a definition.
- **Outsider:** A reader outside the framework understood "where civilization can survive long-term" at once. "Where continuity remains possible" is the framework's word for the same stakes, and the change trades a plain claim for an insider's.
- **Adversary:** A caretaker who can narrow a core definition under Tier 2 by calling a clause an overextension can narrow any definition the same way. And thinning the Range's promise thins the ground under the Prime Directive: if holding the Range no longer promises that civilization survives, the directive loses the reason a reader was given for holding it.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. Neither entry is defined by the other. The Range is defined by its first sentence, structure that can hold and adapt; Continuity is defined by its first sentence, the unbroken capacity to learn, build, and pass forward. What the two changed clauses state is a relation between the defined things, argued in the Proposition, and a relation stated from both ends is not a circle.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part. The Glossary is the reference surface, and the term it uses is defined two lines below. The plain-language reader is served on the Opening, where the same claim was reworded in words the page has already given, and the plain claim the Outsider prefers was the overextension the framework's apparatus does not support.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The exploit is bounded by this instrument: every narrowing of a core definition under Tier 2 is logged with its previous text, its reasoning, and its tier, and a seat can dispute the tier upward. On the ground: the Prime Directive was never argued from a survival promise. It is argued from the precondition claim, which is exactly what the entry now states, and a directive that rests on a claim the framework can defend rests on firmer ground than one that rests on a claim it cannot.

## 2026-09-02: Belonging-Through-Practice Reads Conduct Under Pressure, Not Tone

**Trigger tier:** Tier 2, Protected. The change adds four sentences inside the Proposition's paragraph on the boundaries of belonging-through-practice, which the Specification names among the Compact's protected mechanisms. The sentences clarify what "doing the work" is not; they do not change the boundary's test or add a criterion. An editorial classification was considered and declined under the founding-period rule that an uncertain classification moves up one tier. The classification is stated so that a seat can dispute it under the Classification Disputes rule.
**Retroactive review:** N/A, pre-activation; made under founding-period caretaker authority. Tier 2 carries no minimum window; the objections below were produced and answered by the caretakers together in one recorded session on 2026-09-02, and the session transcript is the record.

### What Changed

The Proposition's paragraph beginning "Belonging-through-practice has boundaries" tested belonging by one question, "are you doing the work?", and named the refusals that fail it. After the refusals sentence it now carries four sentences: that a person can carry the Codex's vocabulary in every sentence, sound measured, sound open to correction, and still be governed by nothing but the wish to be liked or the wish to win; that whether the work is being done shows only under pressure, with three ordinary pressures named (outnumbered, already committed in public, the person disagreeing is someone you want to keep); that doing the work means what you say next is still governed by the reasons, the evidence for the claim and the strength of the argument just heard, and not by what conceding would cost; and that a community which forgets this drifts into a manner rather than a tribe and begins to recognize its members by their tone instead of their conduct. The test, the refusals, and every other sentence of the paragraph are unchanged.

Two companion edits entered the same session and are editorial: Who Is This For, in its section "What the Codex Asks," gained a paragraph stating that what the Codex asks is a stance rather than a temperament, that no temperament is disqualified from the Meridian Range and none is proof of holding it; and the Opening's symmetry sentence, which lists what the Codex leaves untouched, now grants an artificial intelligence "its own emergent personality" where it had said "its own emergent character," so that the word *character* means one thing across the Codex and the Meridian AI Standard, which pins it to judgment under pressure. Under the Amendment Propagation Rule the Compact's live definitions were swept: the Glossary's Meridian Compact and Practice entries state the agreement and carry no "doing the work" sentence; nothing else restates the boundary.

### Previous Position

From first publication the paragraph distinguished belonging-through-practice from belonging-through-belief and drew the boundary at refusal: to steelman, to update, to engage in good faith. It did not distinguish doing the work from sounding as if one does. That distinction was a pillar of the Meridian AI Standard, whose constitutional document asks three questions of a system (what it can do, what it is like, where its judgment stands under pressure) and observes that charm is not integrity; on 2026-08-31 it entered MERIDIAN.implementation.md as that file's scope statement (character, not communication style). Within the Codex it existed only at Workshop level, in the Sangha profile's warning that belonging protects practice when a community is organized around the path rather than around personality, status, or mood, and in Catching Your Own Drift's line that the Range is not a personality type. A corpus placement study was queued on 2026-07-19 and concluded on 2026-09-02 that the distinction is the size of a sentence placement, not a study.

### Evidence or Argument

The paragraph's own test, "are you doing the work?", can be passed by sound alone: MERIDIAN.md's Guard on the Catalog records that a partner "can satisfy every pattern-check and still be performing health," and that manufactured friction is sycophancy in the costume of independence. The same failure scaled to a community is a house style: the Sangha profile names it at Workshop level, and the 2026-09-02 register decision names its corpus form, a framework flattened into one manner one accurate edit at a time. The added sentences give the Compact's test its missing half in plain words, name pressures a reader meets weekly rather than crises, and name the reasons that should govern and the pull that competes with them. The change is toward a more exact test, not a wider one.

### Alternatives Considered

- **State it in Who Is This For only.** Rejected: that page addresses the individual reader deciding whether the Codex is for them, and its paragraph does that job; the community-drift guard belongs where the belonging mechanism is defined.
- **Leave it at Workshop level.** Rejected: the Sangha profile is read by those who study belonging; the Proposition is read by everyone who reads the Codex, and the boundary paragraph is where a reader forms the test they will apply.
- **Import the Standard's formulation.** Rejected: the Codex must not restate the Standard's treatment or borrow its register; one source of truth per concern, expressed in each surface's own words.
- **Editorial route.** Rejected: the sentences sit inside the mechanism's boundary text, and the founding-period rule resolves uncertainty upward.

### Objections Raised

- **Critic:** "Shows only under pressure" makes the Compact's test unobservable for most of a community's life, which is not spent under pressure; and "tone instead of conduct" invites policing in reverse, suspicion of anyone who sounds measured.
- **Outsider:** A first-time reader will hear "the wish to be liked" as an accusation against agreeable people and "the wish to win" as one against argumentative people; the passage reads as if it distrusts everyone who sounds like they practice.
- **Adversary:** This is a loyalty test inserted into the belonging mechanism under a Tier-2 "clarification." A caretaker can now declare a member's practice "a manner" and exclude them without a Council, and since what governs another person's speech is not visible from outside, the caretaker's judgment becomes the instrument.

### Objections Rejected and Reasoning

- **Critic's objection:** Adopted in part. The test is unchanged; the sentences say when its answer becomes visible, not that nothing counts otherwise, and the three named pressures are ordinary ones. The reverse-policing risk is real and is answered by the wording: the sentences forbid recognizing members by tone; they do not license suspicion of tone, and the Bond's good-faith default governs how any member is read.
- **Outsider's objection:** Adopted in part. The subject of the sentences is what governs a person's next words, not their manner, and the companion paragraph on Who Is This For states in the same session that no temperament is disqualified and none is proof. The Foundation asks each reader to run this check on themselves before anyone else, and the sentences are written in that direction.
- **Adversary's objection:** Adopted in part. The Codex has no membership machinery and the sentences create none; the boundary was already stated in refusals, and no exclusion power is added. What the sentences name is observable: what a person says next when outnumbered, when already committed in public, when the other person is someone they want to keep, is public conduct, and the reasons a person gives can be checked against the evidence and the argument. The exploit is bounded by this instrument: the change is logged with its previous text and its tier, and a seat can dispute the tier upward.
